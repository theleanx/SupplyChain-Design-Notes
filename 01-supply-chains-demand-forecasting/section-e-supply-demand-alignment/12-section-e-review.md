# 12. Section E Review and Original Practice

These original learning exercises reinforce the section's concepts through practical scenarios.

## Rapid review

You should now be able to explain:

- the operations planning and control hierarchy;
- strategic vs. business vs. master vs. resource planning;
- the monthly S&OP cycle;
- demand review, supply review, financial review, pre-S&OP, and executive S&OP;
- product-family planning;
- level, chase, and hybrid production;
- MTS, MTO, ETO, ATO, and PTO;
- demand prioritization, time fences, allocation, and customer-service measures.

## Original knowledge checks

### 1. Planning level

A company is deciding whether to purchase a new furnace that requires 14 months to order, install, and qualify. Which planning activity should identify this need?

A. Material requirements planning  
B. Resource planning  
C. Production activity control  
D. Final assembly scheduling

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B — Resource planning.** The decision concerns long-lead capacity. MRP and execution planning operate at much more detailed, shorter-term levels.
</details>

### 2. Aggregate planning

The executive team is reviewing monthly demand and supply for three product families over the next 15 months. Which process best fits this activity?

A. S&OP  
B. Dispatching  
C. Cycle counting  
D. Detailed finite scheduling

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A — S&OP.** The horizon and product-family level indicate aggregate tactical planning.
</details>

### 3. Demand review

Marketing and sales disagree on expected demand for a product family after a campaign. What is the best S&OP response?

A. Keep separate official forecasts  
B. Let operations choose whichever number it prefers  
C. Document assumptions and agree one consensus demand number  
D. Escalate every SKU directly to the CEO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The purpose of the demand review is to establish one supported demand view and document assumptions.
</details>

### 4. Supply review

Demand is 5% above normal capacity for one quarter. Which is the best first supply-review action?

A. Immediately cut the demand plan  
B. Develop feasible capacity and timing alternatives with costs and risks  
C. Ignore the gap until customer orders arrive  
D. Increase finished-goods inventory after the quarter ends

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Supply review should identify alternatives before treating demand reduction as the default solution.
</details>

### 5. Level strategy

A manufacturer keeps output nearly constant while inventory rises before its peak season and falls during the peak. Which strategy is being used?

A. Chase  
B. Level  
C. ETO  
D. PTO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B — Level.** Production remains stable while inventory absorbs demand variation.
</details>

### 6. Chase strategy

Which situation best supports a chase approach?

A. Inventory is cheap but workforce changes are very expensive  
B. Perishable output and highly flexible staffing  
C. Demand is perfectly stable  
D. Capacity cannot be changed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Chase is more attractive when inventory is costly or impossible to hold and capacity can change economically.
</details>

### 7. ATO

A manufacturer stocks standard motors, housings, controllers, and seals, then performs final configuration after receiving the customer's option selection. Which environment is this?

A. MTS  
B. ATO  
C. ETO  
D. PTO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B — Assemble-to-order.** Common modules are stocked and final assembly/configuration occurs after the order.
</details>

### 8. ETO

A customer order requires a new engineering design, unique BOM, and newly sourced materials. Which environment applies?

A. MTS  
B. ATO  
C. ETO  
D. PTO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C — Engineer-to-order.** Engineering itself is part of fulfilling the specific order.
</details>

### 9. PTO

The physical product is standard, but language-specific labels and package quantities are completed only after the destination order is known. Which approach is this?

A. PTO  
B. Chase production  
C. MRP  
D. Level production

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A — Package-to-order.** The common product is available before the order while packaging is postponed.
</details>

### 10. Pre-S&OP

What is the strongest purpose of pre-S&OP?

A. Replace the executive meeting  
B. Resolve what can be resolved and prepare decision-ready issues for executives  
C. Create individual SKU schedules  
D. Perform cycle counting

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Pre-S&OP narrows unresolved cross-functional issues into choices that need executive authority.
</details>

### 11. Financial review

Operations can cover a demand spike through either overtime or subcontracting. What should finance primarily add to the S&OP process?

A. A new statistical forecast  
B. Financial impact and fit with business-plan constraints  
C. A detailed routing file  
D. Customer segmentation by demographics

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Finance translates alternatives into revenue, cost, cash, profit, and capital implications.
</details>

### 12. Product-family level

Why is S&OP normally reviewed by product family rather than individual SKU?

A. Families eliminate the need for forecasting  
B. Aggregate planning makes enterprise volume and capacity trade-offs manageable  
C. Individual products never share resources  
D. Finance cannot work with SKU data

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** S&OP focuses on aggregate tactical trade-offs; detailed item planning occurs later.
</details>

### 13. Resource planning vs. RCCP

Which statement is most accurate?

A. Resource planning checks long-lead aggregate capacity; RCCP checks key resources against the MPS later  
B. They are identical  
C. RCCP always occurs before S&OP  
D. Resource planning is a shop-floor dispatching tool

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The two checks occur at different planning levels and time horizons.
</details>

### 14. Demand prioritization

A shortage exists and individual salespeople each want their own customers served first. What is the best control?

A. Let each salesperson reserve inventory  
B. Use an enterprise prioritization policy with defined authority and criteria  
C. Use first-come-first-served for every situation  
D. Stop measuring customer service

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Scarce supply should be allocated according to enterprise priorities, not local incentives.
</details>

### 15. Time fences

Why can a time fence support demand prioritization?

A. It permanently prevents all changes  
B. It identifies decision points after which schedule changes become increasingly disruptive or costly  
C. It replaces the forecast  
D. It increases product variety

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Time fences protect committed operations while allowing earlier periods to remain more flexible.
</details>

### 16. Fill-rate interpretation

An order has 20 lines. Eighteen are completely filled. What is the line-item fill rate?

A. 80%  
B. 85%  
C. 90%  
D. 95%

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C — 90%.** 18 ÷ 20 = 0.90.
</details>

### 17. Consensus

Which statement best describes S&OP consensus?

A. Every department keeps its own number but agrees not to argue  
B. Finance chooses the number  
C. Functions understand the assumptions and support one approved enterprise plan  
D. The statistical forecast can never be changed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Consensus is one approved plan with shared understanding and commitment.
</details>

### 18. Implementation

A company holds monthly S&OP meetings, but sales continues to promise above the approved plan and finance uses a separate forecast. What is the fundamental problem?

A. Too few meetings  
B. S&OP has not become the unified operating process  
C. Too much resource planning  
D. The company needs daily executive meetings

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Meeting cadence alone does not create S&OP; functions must actually execute against the agreed plan.
</details>

### 19. One-time opportunity

A highly profitable one-time order would consume scarce capacity and delay regular customers. What is the best response?

A. Accept it immediately because revenue is high  
B. Reject it immediately because it is unplanned  
C. Evaluate incremental profit, capacity alternatives, strategic-customer impact, and prioritization options  
D. Hide it from S&OP until the next quarter

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The exception should be evaluated from an enterprise profit and customer-service perspective.
</details>

### 20. Customer service metric

A distributor cares most about having every requested product line available, even when the quantities on each line are small. Which measure is most directly relevant?

A. Monetary-value fill rate  
B. Line-item fill rate  
C. Machine utilization  
D. Forecast bias

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Line-item fill rate measures whether requested order lines are fulfilled.
</details>

## Final memory anchors

- **Strategy sets direction; S&OP turns it into an aggregate executable plan.**
- **Demand review agrees what is needed.**
- **Supply review tests what can be supported.**
- **Finance tests what makes economic sense.**
- **Executive S&OP makes the enterprise trade-offs.**
- **Level stabilizes production; chase follows demand; hybrid combines both.**
- **MTS stocks finished goods; ATO stocks modules; MTO waits for the order; ETO engineers to the order; PTO delays packaging.**
- **Prioritize scarce supply according to enterprise policy, not local incentives.**
