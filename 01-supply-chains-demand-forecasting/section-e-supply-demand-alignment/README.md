# Section E: Supply and Demand Alignment / S&OP

Supply and demand alignment turns separate functional plans into **one coordinated operating direction**. This section connects strategy, the business plan, demand planning, production planning, resource planning, finance, and execution through sales and operations planning (S&OP).

The central idea is simple: **sales should sell to a plan that supply can support, supply should produce to the same plan, and finance should confirm that the plan makes business sense.**

## Learning outcomes

After completing Section E, you should be able to:

- place strategic, business, demand, production, resource, master, and execution plans on one hierarchy;
- explain the purpose and monthly flow of S&OP;
- prepare demand- and supply-review evidence and reconcile the financial consequence;
- compare level, chase, and hybrid operating strategies;
- distinguish MTS, MTO, ETO, ATO, and PTO response environments;
- design implementation and stakeholder-buy-in controls; and
- apply customer-service and demand-prioritization rules when supply is constrained.

## Recurring case study

The worked examples use **NorthStar Industrial Systems**, a fictional industrial-pump manufacturer. All volumes, prices, costs, capacity figures, and scenarios are illustrative and independently created for learning.

## How to study the section

Use the section as a closed planning loop: demand review → supply review → reconciliation → executive decision → execution feedback. Test each strategy or prioritization rule against service, capacity, inventory, and financial consequences rather than treating S&OP as a meeting sequence.
