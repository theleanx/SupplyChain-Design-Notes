# 14. Section A Review

Use these independently authored questions to test logistics design, warehousing, materials handling, and transportation judgment. Choose an answer before opening the rationale.

## Question 1

**Question.** HarborPeak can reduce freight by $90,000 but would add $130,000 of inventory and failure exposure. Which decision principle applies?

A. Choose lower freight because transport owns that budget

B. Use the total logistics cost at the required service level

C. Choose fastest delivery regardless of economics

D. Move the cost to warehousing

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Use the total logistics cost at the required service level**

### Why it is correct

The total cost concept compares combined logistics effects rather than one functional expense.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** This looks only at the transportation budget and ignores the larger inventory and failure-exposure increase.

- **C.** Premium speed is not automatically valuable; the required service level and total-system economics still govern.

- **D.** Moving a cost to another function changes accounting ownership, not the supply-chain economic effect.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** Which statement best describes a 4PL-style role?

A. It only owns trucks

B. It coordinates multiple logistics providers for the client

C. It is a fixed warehouse location

D. It is a transportation rate class

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. It coordinates multiple logistics providers for the client**

### Why it is correct

The distinguishing role is integrated orchestration of logistics providers.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Owning trucks describes an asset model, not the defining integrated-management role of a 4PL.

- **C.** A fixed warehouse location is a facility choice, not a logistics-orchestration role.

- **D.** Carrier rate classes describe transportation-market structure, not multi-provider logistics integration.

</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** What is the strongest reason to apply provider capability gates before price weighting?

A. To make every provider equally expensive

B. To prevent an infeasible low-cost option from winning

C. To eliminate service KPIs

D. To avoid documenting requirements

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. To prevent an infeasible low-cost option from winning**

### Why it is correct

Hard requirements must pass before a weighted commercial comparison is meaningful.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Capability gates do not exist to normalize price; they screen whether an alternative can perform the required job.

- **C.** Removing service KPIs would weaken provider governance rather than protect the decision.

- **D.** Capability screening depends on documented requirements and evidence, so avoiding documentation defeats the control.

</details>

## Question 4

**Question.** When can risk pooling reduce aggregate inventory?

A. When demand is aggregated across markets or products so variability can offset

B. Whenever another warehouse is opened

C. Only when every customer demand pattern is identical

D. Only when transportation cost is zero

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. When demand is aggregated across markets or products so variability can offset**

### Why it is correct

Pooling can reduce the combined variability that separate local buffers would cover.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Opening another warehouse can fragment demand and inventory; it does not by itself create pooling.

- **C.** Pooling is valuable precisely because different demand streams need not move identically for variability to offset.

- **D.** Transportation cost affects the overall trade-off, but zero transport cost is not a condition for the statistical pooling effect.

</details>

## Question 5

**Question.** What is a key requirement for random-location storage?

A. No warehouse system

B. An accurate locator record/WMS discipline

C. A permanent maximum-sized location for every SKU

D. Only one SKU in the warehouse

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. An accurate locator record/WMS discipline**

### Why it is correct

Dynamic locations require exact system control of item position.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Random storage needs stronger location information, not the absence of a warehouse system.

- **C.** A permanent maximum-sized location describes fixed-location logic and gives up much of random storage’s cube benefit.

- **D.** Random-location storage can manage many SKUs; it is not restricted to a one-SKU warehouse.

</details>

## Question 6

**Question.** Why can adding warehouses eventually raise total logistics cost?

A. Customer service always worsens

B. Inventory and facility overhead can be duplicated across more nodes

C. Transportation distance always increases

D. Every warehouse must be privately owned

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Inventory and facility overhead can be duplicated across more nodes**

### Why it is correct

Additional facilities can add redundant inventory, labor, equipment, and overhead.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** More warehouses can improve response for some customers; service does not necessarily worsen.

- **C.** Average transport distance can fall when inventory is closer to customers, even while other costs rise.

- **D.** The total-cost effect comes from node duplication and economics, not from a requirement that every facility be privately owned.

</details>

## Question 7

**Question.** Which location factor should normally be treated as a feasibility gate rather than a preference?

A. Required truck/rail access for the operating design

B. A slightly lower landscaping cost

C. Manager preference for a neighborhood

D. A cosmetic building feature

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Required truck/rail access for the operating design**

### Why it is correct

If required transportation access is absent, the site may be unable to perform the logistics role.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Landscaping cost may affect economics but usually does not determine whether the logistics operation can physically function.

- **C.** Manager preference is not an operating feasibility requirement.

- **D.** Cosmetic building features do not replace required access, utilities, or other operating infrastructure.

</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** HarborPeak's vertical-adjusted area is 6,000 m² and target utilization is 80%. What core area is required before bulk space?

A. 4,800 m²

B. 6,000 m²

C. 7,500 m²

D. 8,000 m²

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. 7,500 m²**

### Why it is correct

6,000 / 0.80 = 7,500 m².

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Multiplying by 80% understates the space; target utilization requires dividing the operating requirement by 0.80.

- **B.** 6,000 m² is the already-adjusted operating area and contains no utilization headroom.

- **D.** 8,000 m² would imply a different utilization assumption; it does not follow from 6,000 ÷ 0.80.

</details>

**Question.** Which equipment is most directly associated with moving very heavy or awkward loads beyond normal forklift capability?

A. Bridge/wagon crane

B. Pick-to-light only

C. Manual document cart

D. Parcel label printer

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Bridge/wagon crane**

### Why it is correct

Crane-type equipment is designed for heavy lifting and awkward loads.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Pick-to-light guides picking but does not physically lift very heavy or awkward loads.

- **C.** A document cart is not heavy-load material-handling equipment.

- **D.** A parcel label printer creates shipping labels; it does not move material.

</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** What is the main risk of automating an unstable, frequently changing process?

A. Automation always lowers fixed cost

B. The system can hard-code a poor or inflexible process

C. It eliminates the need for data

D. It guarantees zero downtime

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. The system can hard-code a poor or inflexible process**

### Why it is correct

High fixed automation can reduce flexibility and embed bad process logic.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Automation normally increases fixed commitment rather than guaranteeing lower fixed cost.

- **C.** Automated systems depend on accurate data and control logic rather than eliminating that need.

- **D.** Automation can fail mechanically or digitally; zero downtime is not guaranteed.

</details>

## Question 11

**Question.** What is transportation's primary objective in this section?

A. Permanent inventory storage

B. Product movement between supply-chain nodes

C. Replacing forecasting

D. Eliminating warehousing

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Product movement between supply-chain nodes**

### Why it is correct

The primary role is movement; temporary storage is secondary.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Temporary storage can occur during transportation, but it is secondary rather than the primary objective.

- **C.** Transportation planning consumes forecasts; it does not replace forecasting.

- **D.** Transportation and warehousing are complementary logistics functions rather than substitutes that eliminate each other.

</details>

## Question 12

**Question.** A full truckload lowers per-unit freight cost but forces orders to wait longer. What trade-off is being created?

A. Transportation vs. inventory/lead time

B. Quality vs. accounting

C. Customs vs. production routing

D. Warehouse ownership vs. fixed storage

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Transportation vs. inventory/lead time**

### Why it is correct

Consolidation can improve freight economics while increasing time and inventory.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** The situation concerns shipment consolidation and waiting time, not a quality-versus-accounting choice.

- **C.** Customs and production routing are different processes and do not describe this consolidation trade-off.

- **D.** Warehouse ownership and fixed storage do not explain the extra wait created by building a fuller transport load.

</details>

## Question 13

**Question.** What does economy of distance imply?

A. Longer trips always cost less in total

B. Cost per mile can decline as fixed/start-stop effects are spread over more distance

C. Distance has no effect on transportation

D. Only air transportation has fixed costs

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Cost per mile can decline as fixed/start-stop effects are spread over more distance**

### Why it is correct

Total cost generally rises with distance, but not proportionally per mile.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Total trip cost normally increases with distance; the economy is a lower cost per distance unit, not a lower total cost.

- **C.** Distance affects fuel, labor, equipment time, and fixed/start-stop cost spreading.

- **D.** Fixed/start-stop cost effects exist across transportation modes, not only air.

</details>

## Question 14

**Question.** A shipment is awkwardly shaped and wastes cargo space. Which transportation factor is most directly affected?

A. Stowability

B. Forecast bias

C. Warehouse ownership

D. Cycle counting

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Stowability**

### Why it is correct

Stowability describes how efficiently the item shares cargo space.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Forecast bias concerns systematic demand-estimate error, not how cargo uses vehicle cube.

- **C.** Warehouse ownership does not describe the physical fit of a shipment in transport equipment.

- **D.** Cycle counting checks inventory-record accuracy, not cargo-space utilization.

</details>

## Question 15

**Question.** HarborPeak forecasts 705 units on a lane and has 690 reserved. What is the capacity gap?

A. +15

B. 0

C. -15

D. -705

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. -15**

### Why it is correct

Reserved minus required = 690 - 705 = -15 units.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A positive 15 would reverse the subtraction and incorrectly imply extra reserved capacity.

- **B.** The lane is not balanced because required capacity exceeds the reservation.

- **D.** The gap is the difference between reserved and required capacity, not the negative of the full demand.

</details>

## Question 16

**Question.** Why should transportation forecasts be built by lane and mode?

A. Because company-wide totals can hide local shortages

B. Because every lane has the same constraints

C. Because modes never change

D. Because forecasting eliminates carrier contracts

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Because company-wide totals can hide local shortages**

### Why it is correct

Capacity is often constrained at specific origins, destinations, modes, equipment types, and periods.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Lanes differ in demand, geography, equipment, provider availability, and constraints; treating them as identical hides risk.

- **C.** Mode can change and is itself a planning choice, especially when time exists to design intermodal alternatives.

- **D.** Forecasting supports procurement and contracting; it does not eliminate carrier agreements.

</details>

## Question 17

**Question.** Which action best represents information substituting for inventory?

A. Holding duplicate stock because location data is unreliable

B. Using accurate visibility, postponement, or cross-docking to reduce unnecessary buffers

C. Removing all physical inventory

D. Adding another warehouse without reviewing demand

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Using accurate visibility, postponement, or cross-docking to reduce unnecessary buffers**

### Why it is correct

Better information can reduce uncertainty-driven buffers without claiming that all physical stock disappears.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Duplicate stock caused by unreliable data is an example of uncertainty creating more inventory, not information replacing it.

- **C.** Information can reduce buffers, but it cannot eliminate all physical inventory needed for service and operations.

- **D.** Adding a warehouse without demand evidence adds capacity and complexity rather than substituting information for inventory.

</details>

## Question 18

**Question.** Which statement about private, public, and contract warehousing is most accurate?

A. One type is always cheapest

B. They trade off control, flexibility, service tailoring, and fixed commitment

C. Public warehouses cannot provide logistics services

D. Private warehouses have no fixed cost

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. They trade off control, flexibility, service tailoring, and fixed commitment**

### Why it is correct

Ownership structure changes control, flexibility, economics, and service tailoring; there is no universal winner.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** No ownership type is universally cheapest because cost, utilization, control, and service conditions vary.

- **C.** Public/for-hire warehouse providers can perform storage and related logistics services.

- **D.** Private warehousing carries fixed asset and operating commitments rather than having no fixed cost.

</details>
