# Supply Chain Design Glossary

This glossary provides concise working definitions for terms used across the repository. Definitions support practical planning discussions; individual lessons provide context, limitations, and examples.

## A

**ABC classification** — A method of grouping inventory items by annual monetary value or another approved criterion so control effort can be concentrated where it matters most.

**Available-to-promise (ATP)** — The uncommitted portion of inventory and planned production that can support new customer promises under the stated master-scheduling rules.


**Aggregate plan** — A medium-term plan expressed at a product-family or resource level rather than at individual item level.

**Application programming interface (API)** — A defined interface through which software applications request data or actions from one another.

**Assemble-to-order (ATO)** — A production approach in which common components are prepared in advance and configured into a finished product after demand is known.

**Associative forecasting** — Forecasting that estimates demand using one or more variables believed to influence it.

**Asset turnover** — Revenue divided by average assets, indicating how much revenue the asset base generates.

**Automatic identification and data capture** — Technologies that identify an object and record data with limited manual entry, including barcodes and radio-frequency identification.

## B

**Backorder** — Confirmed customer demand that cannot be fulfilled by the requested date and remains open for later delivery.

**Benchmarking** — Comparing a measure or process with a relevant reference point to identify a performance gap and improvement question.

**Bias** — A persistent forecasting tendency to be predominantly above or below actual demand.

**Bullwhip effect** — Amplification of demand variation as orders and information move upstream through a supply network.

**BATNA** — The best feasible course of action available if a negotiation does not produce an agreement.

## C

**Capacity** — The output a resource, work center, facility, or supply network can produce during a defined period.

**Capacity requirements planning (CRP)** — A detailed, time-phased check of work-center load created by planned and released orders against available capacity.

**Cycle counting** — A recurring inventory-accuracy method that counts selected items on a schedule rather than relying only on a full annual physical inventory.


**Cash-to-cash time** — Inventory days plus receivable days minus payable days, estimating how long operating cash remains tied up.

**Chase strategy** — An operations strategy that adjusts capacity or output to follow changes in demand.

**Coefficient of correlation** — A measure of the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables.

**Competitive intelligence** — Systematic collection and interpretation of information about competitors, markets, and strategic moves.

**Constraint** — A resource, rule, material, capability, or policy that limits the feasible plan.

**Control tower** — A capability that combines network events, context, exceptions, and response workflows to improve cross-functional decisions.

**Cycle** — A wave-like demand movement extending beyond a normal seasonal pattern and often associated with broader business conditions.

**Cybersecurity** — Protection of systems, identities, data, and operations against unauthorized access, disruption, alteration, or misuse.

**Category strategy** — A coordinated plan for managing a group of related external spend using shared demand, market, supplier, risk, and value actions.

**Contract deployment** — Translation of signed commercial obligations into owners, processes, systems, data, training, measures, and escalation controls.

## D

**Demand management** — The coordinated process of recognizing, planning, communicating, influencing, and prioritizing demand.

**Demand pattern** — The observable combination of level, trend, seasonality, cycles, events, and random variation in demand history.

**Demand plan** — The organization's agreed statement of expected demand after considering statistical forecasts, market intelligence, commercial actions, and assumptions.

**Demand shaping** — Deliberate action to influence the timing, mix, volume, or profitability of demand.

**Digital thread** — The connected flow of identifiers, events, decisions, and records across a product or order lifecycle.

**Deseasonalizing** — Removing an estimated seasonal effect so that the underlying demand level or trend can be analyzed.

## E

**Economic order quantity (EOQ)** — A fixed-order-quantity model that balances ordering or setup cost against inventory carrying cost under stated simplifying assumptions.


**Engineer-to-order (ETO)** — A production approach in which significant engineering work begins after a specific customer requirement is received.

**Environmental scan** — Structured review of external political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental forces.

**Event latency** — Elapsed time between a real-world event and its usable arrival in a decision process.

**Exponential smoothing** — A forecasting method that updates the prior forecast using a smoothing factor applied to the most recent error.

## F

**Fill rate** — The percentage of requested demand fulfilled immediately from available supply, usually measured by units, lines, or orders.

**First-pass yield** — The proportion of units that complete a process correctly without rework, repair, or repeat processing.

**Forecast** — An estimate of future demand based on available evidence and stated assumptions.

**Forecast error** — The difference between actual demand and forecast demand for the same period.

**Forecast horizon** — The future time span covered by a forecast.

## G

**Greenhouse-gas emissions intensity** — Emissions associated with a defined unit of activity, such as a shipment, tonne-kilometre, or unit of revenue.

## H

**Hybrid strategy** — An operations strategy that combines level output, capacity adjustments, inventory, backlog, external supply, or demand shaping.

## I

**Integration** — The controlled exchange of data or actions among applications, organizations, or processes.

**Inventory turnover** — Annualized cost of goods sold divided by average inventory, indicating how frequently inventory cycles through the business.

## J

**Just-in-time (JIT)** — An operating philosophy that seeks to supply only what is needed, when it is needed, while reducing waste, variability, queues, lead time, and defects.

## K

**Kanban** — A visual or electronic pull signal authorizing replenishment or movement only when downstream use creates a need.

## L

**Lagging indicator** — A measure that confirms a change after the underlying business condition has already shifted.

**Lateral integration** — Coordination or combination with organizations operating at a similar stage of a value chain.

**Leading indicator** — A measure that may signal a future change before it appears in the primary outcome.

**Level strategy** — An operations strategy that keeps output relatively stable and absorbs demand variation through inventory, backlog, or other buffers.

**Landed cost** — The purchase or production cost plus the logistics, duty, brokerage, handling, and other costs required to place supply at the specified destination.

## M

**Master production schedule (MPS)** — The item-level, time-phased statement of what end items or master-scheduled items are planned for completion.

**Material requirements planning (MRP)** — Time-phased planning logic that uses the MPS, bills of material, inventory status, lead times, and lot-sizing rules to calculate dependent-demand requirements and planned orders.


**Make-to-order (MTO)** — A production approach in which product completion is initiated by a specific customer order.

**Make-to-stock (MTS)** — A production approach in which finished goods are produced in advance based on expected demand.

**Master data** — Relatively stable records, such as items, customers, suppliers, and locations, that give transactions shared meaning.

**Mean absolute deviation (MAD)** — The average absolute forecast error across a defined set of periods.

**Mean absolute percentage error (MAPE)** — The average absolute forecast error expressed as a percentage of actual demand.

**Mean squared error (MSE)** — The average of squared forecast errors, which gives greater weight to larger misses.

**Moving average** — A forecast calculated from the average of a selected number of recent observations.

**Mean time between failures (MTBF)** — Average operating time between repairable equipment failures.

**Mean time to repair (MTTR)** — Average elapsed time required to restore failed equipment to service.

**Make-or-buy decision** — A strategic choice about whether a defined capability, product, component, process, or service should be performed internally, externally, or through a hybrid model.

**Modular design** — A product architecture with governed interfaces that allows components or subassemblies to be reused, configured, replaced, or upgraded with controlled independence.

## N

**New-product introduction (NPI)** — The coordinated process of preparing a new product for commercial launch and operational execution.

## O

**Operations plan** — A time-phased statement of the output, inventory, workforce, capacity, and service response required to support the agreed plan.

**Order penetration point** — The point in the supply process where a specific customer order becomes linked to materials or capacity.

**Offshoring** — Locating work in a different country, whether the work remains internally owned or is performed by an external organization.

**Outsourcing** — Assigning a defined activity, capability, product, or service to an external provider.

## P

**PDCA** — Plan–Do–Check–Act, a recurring cycle for testing action, evaluating results, and improving the next decision.

**Perfect order** — An order that satisfies every included service condition, commonly completeness, timeliness, damage-free delivery, and documentation accuracy.

**Planning horizon** — The future interval over which plans, demand, supply, and constraints are evaluated.

**Projected available balance (PAB)** — The projected inventory remaining after applying planned supply and the demand basis used for each master-schedule period.


**Postponement** — Delaying final product differentiation or commitment until more reliable demand information is available.

**Product life cycle** — The progression of a product through introduction, growth, maturity, and decline, with different demand and supply implications at each stage.

**Product portfolio** — The collection of products, services, families, and variants managed as a set of strategic and economic choices.

**Production-to-order (PTO)** — A broad order-driven approach in which production activity is authorized in response to customer demand.

**Purchase order** — An authorized commercial document stating the item or service, quantity, price, delivery, payment, and applicable execution terms.

## R

**Random variation** — Demand movement that cannot be explained reliably by known patterns or drivers.

**Regression** — A statistical method that estimates the relationship between an outcome and one or more explanatory variables.

**Return on assets (ROA)** — Profit divided by average assets; it can also be viewed as profit margin multiplied by asset turnover.

**Radio-frequency identification (RFID)** — Identification technology that uses radio waves to read encoded tags, often without direct line of sight.

**Reseasonalizing** — Reapplying the expected seasonal effect to a forecast of the underlying demand level.

**Resource plan** — A long- or medium-range check that determines whether critical resources can support the business or operations plan.

**Rough-cut capacity planning (RCCP)** — A medium-range capacity check that tests whether critical resources can support a preliminary master production schedule.


## S

**Safety stock** — Inventory maintained to reduce the risk created by demand or supply uncertainty.

**Software as a service (SaaS)** — Software accessed as a provider-operated service, typically through subscription-based commercial terms.

**Sales and operations planning (S&OP)** — A recurring cross-functional process that reconciles demand, supply, financial expectations, risks, and decisions at an aggregate level.

**Seasonality** — A demand pattern that repeats at predictable calendar intervals.

**Service level** — A defined measure of the organization's ability to meet customer demand or avoid a shortage.

**Statistical forecast** — A forecast generated from quantitative patterns or relationships in data before judgmental adjustments.

**Standard cost** — A predetermined expected cost used as a baseline for planning, inventory valuation, or variance analysis.

**Stewardship** — Assigned operational responsibility for maintaining the definition, quality, and authorized use of a data domain.

**Supply chain** — A network of organizations, resources, processes, and flows that creates and delivers value to a customer.

**Supply review** — A cross-functional evaluation of capacity, materials, inventory, constraints, risks, and response options against the demand plan.

**Six Sigma** — A structured improvement approach that uses data and statistical thinking to reduce process variation and defects; DMAIC is a common improvement cycle.


**SWOT** — A structured view of internal strengths and weaknesses alongside external opportunities and threats.

**Should-cost estimate** — A driver-based estimate of the resources and economics reasonably expected to deliver a defined requirement under stated assumptions.

**Supplier segmentation** — Classification of a supplier relationship using category dependence, supplier capability, mutual attractiveness, and the governance needed to create value.

## T

**Tracking signal** — A ratio comparing cumulative forecast error with MAD to identify sustained bias.

**Takt time** — Available production time divided by customer demand, representing the pace required to match demand; it is not the same as actual cycle time.

**Theory of constraints (TOC)** — A system-improvement approach that identifies the limiting constraint, exploits it, subordinates other work to it, elevates it when necessary, and repeats after the constraint moves.

**Total productive maintenance (TPM)** — A maintenance approach that combines preventive maintenance with broad employee involvement to improve equipment effectiveness, stability, and flow.


**Total cost of ownership (TCO)** — The full lifecycle cost of acquiring, implementing, operating, supporting, changing, and retiring a capability.

**Traceability** — The ability to follow an object's identity, location, transformation, and custody through recorded events.

**Transportation management system (TMS)** — An application that supports transport planning, tendering, execution, settlement, and performance analysis.

**Trend** — A persistent long-term upward or downward movement in demand.

**Three-way match** — A payment control comparing the authorized purchase order, receipt or acceptance evidence, and supplier invoice.

**Total cost of ownership** — The relevant acquisition, transition, logistics, inventory, quality, administration, risk, use, service, and disposition consequences evaluated over a stated horizon.

## V

**Value flow** — The progression through which activities, capabilities, and exchanges increase usefulness for the customer and participating organizations.

**Vertical integration** — Ownership or control of additional upstream or downstream stages in a value chain.

## W

**Warehouse management system (WMS)** — An application that directs and records receiving, storage, picking, packing, and shipping activity within a facility.

**Working capital** — Short-term operating resources and obligations; supply-chain analysis often focuses on inventory, receivables, and payables.

**Work center** — A defined production resource or group of similar resources used for scheduling, routing, load, and capacity control.
