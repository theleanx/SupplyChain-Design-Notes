## Question 1

**Question.** A warehouse KPI improves its local picking cost but increases split shipments and customer lead time. What does this show?

A. The warehouse metric must be a KPI because it improved.

B. Local optimization can conflict with end-to-end performance.

C. Customer lead time is never a supply-chain metric.

D. The KPI should remove all balancing measures.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Local optimization can conflict with end-to-end performance.**

### Why it is correct

A local measure can improve while the end-to-end outcome gets worse. A supply-chain KPI needs balancing measures so cost, service, and flow are not optimized in isolation.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Improvement in one local number does not automatically make it a strategic KPI.
- **C.** Customer lead time can be an important end-to-end service measure.
- **D.** Removing balancing measures would make suboptimization more likely, not less.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** Which statement best distinguishes a KPI from a metric?

A. Every metric is automatically a KPI.

B. A KPI is a metric selected because it represents progress on an important objective.

C. KPIs must be financial.

D. Metrics cannot have targets.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. A KPI is a metric selected because it represents progress on an important objective.**

### Why it is correct

A KPI is a deliberately selected metric tied to a material objective. Organizations can measure many things, but only a limited set should be treated as key indicators of strategic performance.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Many metrics are useful operational measures without being key performance indicators.
- **C.** KPIs may be financial or nonfinancial.
- **D.** Ordinary metrics can also have baselines, targets, and action thresholds.

</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** A system says 500 units are available, but the physical count finds 472. What should happen after protecting immediate service?

A. Ignore the physical count.

B. Correct the record with an authorized adjustment and investigate the cause.

C. Raise the sales forecast by 28.

D. Delete the SKU.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Correct the record with an authorized adjustment and investigate the cause.**

### Why it is correct

The record must be reconciled to verified physical evidence through an authorized adjustment, and the transaction or control failure that caused the difference must be investigated so it does not repeat.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Ignoring the physical evidence leaves replenishment and promise logic based on a false balance.
- **C.** A forecast is a demand estimate; changing it does not correct an inventory-record error.
- **D.** Deleting the SKU destroys the master-data object rather than correcting the quantity and root cause.

</details>

## Question 4

**Question.** A product meets every manufacturing specification but customers dislike an essential feature. Which quality dimension failed?

A. Quality of design.

B. Quality of conformance only.

C. Inventory accuracy.

D. RCCP.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Quality of design.**

### Why it is correct

The product conforms to its manufacturing requirements, but the requirements themselves do not satisfy the customer. That is a design-quality problem.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Conformance is not the primary failure because the product is meeting the stated specification.
- **C.** Inventory accuracy concerns the agreement between recorded and physical stock, not product desirability.
- **D.** RCCP checks critical-resource feasibility for the master schedule and does not define product features.

</details>

## Question 5

**Question.** An improvement team has not documented the current process but already chose automation as the solution. Which CI discipline is being skipped?

A. Current-state process analysis and evidence-based assessment.

B. EOQ calculation.

C. Demand time fence.

D. Cycle counting.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Current-state process analysis and evidence-based assessment.**

### Why it is correct

Continuous improvement should first understand the as-is process, facts, failure pattern, and root causes. Selecting automation before that evidence risks automating waste or the wrong process.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** EOQ is a replenishment lot-size model and does not establish the current-state process.
- **C.** A demand time fence controls master-schedule change authority, not CI diagnosis.
- **D.** Cycle counting is an inventory-accuracy control, not the general first step in process redesign.

</details>

## Question 6

**Question.** A firm compares its invoice-approval process with a world-class service company outside its industry. What type of benchmarking is most likely?

A. Competitive benchmarking only.

B. Best-in-class benchmarking.

C. ABC classification.

D. Capacity control.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Best-in-class benchmarking.**

### Why it is correct

Best-in-class benchmarking deliberately looks beyond direct competitors to organizations that perform the process exceptionally well, even in another industry.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Competitive benchmarking uses a direct or close industry competitor as the reference point.
- **C.** ABC classification prioritizes inventory or other populations by relative value or importance.
- **D.** Capacity control compares actual output and load with capacity at the execution level.

</details>

## Question 7

**Question.** A control chart shows predictable variation, but nearly all output is above the customer’s upper specification. What does that imply?

A. The process is acceptable because it is stable.

B. The process can be stable but incapable of meeting the specification.

C. Control and specification limits must be identical.

D. No data are needed.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. The process can be stable but incapable of meeting the specification.**

### Why it is correct

Statistical stability and customer capability are different questions. A stable process can consistently produce output outside the specification and therefore still require improvement or recentering.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Stability only means the variation pattern is predictable; it does not prove conformance to customer limits.
- **C.** Control limits come from process behavior, while specification limits come from requirements; they serve different purposes.
- **D.** The conclusion depends on measured process data and specification evidence, so data remain essential.

</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** A Pareto chart shows one defect family is the largest source of loss. What should follow?

A. Assume the largest category is caused by operator error.

B. Focused root-cause investigation of that priority, with severity overrides as needed.

C. Stop collecting evidence.

D. Treat every category equally regardless of impact.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Focused root-cause investigation of that priority, with severity overrides as needed.**

### Why it is correct

Pareto analysis identifies where investigation can produce the largest likely benefit. The next step is to investigate root causes, while retaining safety, regulatory, or severity gates that may override frequency ranking.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A Pareto bar identifies a category, not its root cause; operator error cannot be assumed.
- **C.** Evidence collection should become more focused, not stop, after a priority is identified.
- **D.** Treating all categories equally defeats the purpose of concentrating effort on the significant few.

</details>

## Question 9

**Question.** Which tool is best for seeing the shape and spread of continuous dimension measurements?

A. Histogram.

B. Affinity diagram.

C. PDPC.

D. Activity network.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Histogram.**

### Why it is correct

A histogram groups continuous measurements into intervals so the team can see distribution shape, central tendency, spread, skew, and unusual patterns.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** An affinity diagram groups qualitative ideas into themes.
- **C.** A process decision program chart anticipates possible failures and countermeasures in a plan.
- **D.** An activity network displays task sequence, dependencies, and critical-path timing.

</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** A scatter plot shows a strong relationship between humidity and defects. What is the strongest conclusion?

A. Humidity is proven as the cause.

B. There is association worth investigating, but causality still must be tested.

C. The chart proves no relationship.

D. The process is in statistical control.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. There is association worth investigating, but causality still must be tested.**

### Why it is correct

A strong scatter relationship is evidence of association and a useful hypothesis for investigation. It does not by itself establish that changing humidity causes the defect rate to change.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Correlation can result from confounding variables or shared causes, so it is not proof of causation.
- **C.** The plotted relationship is evidence against the claim that there is no association.
- **D.** A scatter plot does not determine statistical control over time; a control chart is used for that purpose.

</details>

## Question 11

**Question.** A team has hundreds of comments and wants to discover natural themes before deciding what to measure. Which tool fits?

A. Affinity diagram.

B. Control chart.

C. EOQ.

D. Takt time.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Affinity diagram.**

### Why it is correct

An affinity diagram organizes a large set of qualitative ideas into related themes, making it useful early in problem framing before numerical measurement is designed.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** A control chart needs time-sequenced quantitative process data.
- **C.** EOQ determines an economic replenishment quantity under stated inventory assumptions.
- **D.** Takt time expresses the production pace required to meet demand; it does not organize qualitative ideas.

</details>

## Question 12

**Question.** A project manager needs to know which dependent task sequence controls completion. Which tool fits?

A. Check sheet.

B. Activity network diagram.

C. Histogram.

D. ABC matrix.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Activity network diagram.**

### Why it is correct

An activity network diagram shows task dependencies, parallel work, duration, and the sequence that controls project completion.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A check sheet records occurrences or observations; it does not model task dependencies.
- **C.** A histogram shows the frequency distribution of measured values.
- **D.** ABC analysis prioritizes a population by value or other criteria and does not calculate project sequence.

</details>

## Question 13

**Question.** Which lean action is most directly consistent with customer pull?

A. Run every machine continuously.

B. Release work because the downstream customer/process has signaled need.

C. Build the largest possible batches.

D. Maximize local WIP.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Release work because the downstream customer/process has signaled need.**

### Why it is correct

Pull means replenishing or producing because downstream use has created a need signal, rather than releasing work simply because an upstream resource is available.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Running every machine continuously can create overproduction when downstream demand is absent.
- **C.** Large batches can increase queue time, inventory, and delayed feedback; they are not inherently pull.
- **D.** Maximizing WIP hides flow problems and conflicts with lean's effort to remove excess inventory and waiting.

</details>

## Question 14

**Question.** Which statement about 5S is strongest?

A. It replaces all quality tools.

B. It is a workplace organization and sustainment method that supports lean; it is not the whole lean system.

C. It requires zero inventory immediately.

D. It is a financial valuation method.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. It is a workplace organization and sustainment method that supports lean; it is not the whole lean system.**

### Why it is correct

5S creates an orderly, visible, standardized workplace and a discipline for sustaining it. It is one supporting lean practice, not a complete operating philosophy by itself.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Quality analysis still requires other tools such as control charts, Pareto analysis, and root-cause methods.
- **C.** 5S does not prescribe an immediate zero-inventory policy.
- **D.** 5S concerns workplace organization and discipline, not accounting valuation.

</details>

## Question 15

**Question.** Available production time is 360 minutes and shift demand is 120 units. What is takt time?

A. 0.33 units/minute only.

B. 3 minutes per unit.

C. 120 minutes per unit.

D. 480 minutes per unit.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. 3 minutes per unit.**

### Why it is correct

Takt time equals available production time divided by demand: 360 minutes / 120 units = 3 minutes per unit.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** 0.33 units per minute is the reciprocal rate, not the requested time-per-unit takt expression.
- **C.** 120 minutes per unit incorrectly uses demand as time and ignores the available-time ratio.
- **D.** 480 minutes per unit adds the two numbers instead of dividing available time by demand.

</details>

## Question 16

**Question.** A process cycle time is consistently slower than takt. What does that suggest?

A. The process necessarily has excess capacity.

B. The process cannot support the required demand pace without a change in capability, method, or demand.

C. Takt should be replaced by inventory turns.

D. The customer demand must be ignored.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. The process cannot support the required demand pace without a change in capability, method, or demand.**

### Why it is correct

If actual cycle time exceeds the allowable takt time, the process is producing more slowly than the pace required by customer demand. Capacity, method, staffing, parallelism, or demand must be addressed.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A slower-than-takt process indicates a capacity or flow shortfall, not excess capacity.
- **C.** Inventory turns measure asset velocity and do not replace the demand-pace comparison.
- **D.** Customer demand is the basis of takt and must remain part of the decision unless demand itself is intentionally changed.

</details>

## Question 17

**Question.** In DMAIC, when should the team determine whether the improvement is being sustained?

A. Define.

B. Measure.

C. Improve only.

D. Control.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Control.**

### Why it is correct

The Control phase establishes monitoring, ownership, response rules, and evidence that the improved process remains at the desired level after implementation.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Define frames the problem, customer need, scope, and objective; it does not prove sustainment.
- **B.** Measure establishes baseline performance and reliable data before the solution is implemented.
- **C.** Improve implements and tests solutions, but without Control there is no governed mechanism to sustain the gain.

</details>

## Question 18

**Question.** A Six Sigma team changes the definition of 'defect opportunity' after seeing poor results. What is the main risk?

A. The measure becomes more objective automatically.

B. The performance measure becomes noncomparable and can be gamed.

C. The process becomes lean.

D. The constraint disappears.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. The performance measure becomes noncomparable and can be gamed.**

### Why it is correct

Changing the denominator or defect definition after seeing results breaks comparability and creates an opportunity to manipulate apparent performance. The measurement definition should be governed before judging results.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Changing the definition after results are known generally reduces objectivity rather than improving it.
- **C.** Lean is a broader waste-and-flow philosophy; redefining a Six Sigma measure does not make a process lean.
- **D.** A measurement-definition change does not physically remove the system constraint.

</details>

## Question 19

**Question.** In TOC, why should a nonconstraint not be run faster simply to improve utilization?

A. It always lowers inventory.

B. It can build WIP without increasing system throughput.

C. It removes the need for a buffer.

D. It changes customer demand.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. It can build WIP without increasing system throughput.**

### Why it is correct

System throughput is governed by the constraint. Producing faster at a nonconstraint can only move inventory toward the constraint more quickly, increasing WIP without increasing completed output.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Running a nonconstraint faster tends to increase, not automatically lower, WIP ahead of the constraint.
- **C.** TOC may deliberately protect the constraint with a controlled buffer; higher nonconstraint utilization does not remove that need.
- **D.** Local production speed does not by itself change market demand.

</details>

## Question 20

**Question.** After elevating the current TOC constraint, what is the next step?

A. Stop improving permanently.

B. Run all resources at maximum output.

C. Return to the start of the focusing cycle and identify the new constraint.

D. Eliminate the rope.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Return to the start of the focusing cycle and identify the new constraint.**

### Why it is correct

TOC is iterative. Once a constraint is broken or moved, the system must be reassessed because a different resource, policy, or market factor will now limit throughput.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A system always has a limiting factor, so improvement does not end permanently when one constraint is elevated.
- **B.** Maximizing every local resource can create overproduction and WIP rather than more system throughput.
- **D.** The rope remains a release-control concept tied to the current constraint; it is redesigned as the constraint changes rather than simply discarded.

</details>
