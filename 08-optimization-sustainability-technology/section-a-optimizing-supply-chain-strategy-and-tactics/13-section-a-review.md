# Section A Review — Strategic Fit and Optimization

This independently authored assessment uses fictional AlderGate Motion Products cases to revisit the twelve Section A lessons. It does not reproduce any certification questions or proprietary distractors. Answer each item before expanding the rationale.

## Review learning outcomes

Show that you can connect strategic position to supply-chain capability, distinguish financial, customer and social value, compare capacity-gated network designs, and interpret optimization and discounted cash flows with the correct units and scope.

## Question 1

**Question.** AlderGate's plant improves utilization, but premium customers report missed delivery commitments. What should the strategy team do?

A. Keep the plant KPI as the only success measure

B. Translate the customer promise into cross-functional requirements and evaluate feasible alternatives

C. Immediately discontinue every low-volume product

D. Remove the service promise without estimating its customer value

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Translate the customer promise into cross-functional requirements and evaluate feasible alternatives**

### Why it is correct

The problem is a mismatch between a local efficiency metric and the external promise. Capability, feasibility and economic evidence must guide the redesign.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A plant-only measure conceals the customer failure.
- **C.** Portfolio discontinuation is not justified by this limited evidence.
- **D.** The customer promise may be economically justified and technically achievable.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** Standard couplings have higher total contribution; engineered assemblies have higher contribution per unit. What evidence is needed for a production priority decision?

A. Always allocate to the highest-volume line

B. Prioritize the highest selling price

C. Compare resource constraints, market value, customer service and cost scope

D. Use unit selling price as the only objective

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Compare resource constraints, market value, customer service and cost scope**

### Why it is correct

Unit margin and total contribution answer different questions; scarce-capacity use and customer consequences can change the priority.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Volume is not itself incremental contribution.
- **B.** Selling price omits the cost and resource required.
- **D.** Selling price cannot alone represent total enterprise value.

</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** Two product families have demand CVs of 0.075 and 0.60. What conclusion follows?

A. Relative variability differs, but replenishment policies require service and supply evidence

B. The second family must have eight times as much safety stock

C. A second warehouse is automatically necessary

D. Forecasting for the second family must be stopped

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Relative variability differs, but replenishment policies require service and supply evidence**

### Why it is correct

Coefficient of variation measures standard deviation relative to mean demand, not a direct inventory or facility requirement.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Safety stock depends on the planning model and lead-time uncertainty.
- **C.** Different variability does not by itself require a separate facility.
- **D.** Long-lead decisions can still require forecasts and forward planning.

</details>

## Question 4

**Question.** AlderGate's hypothetical program has $420,000 annual incremental revenue, $250,000 variable expense and $116,000 dedicated fixed support. What is supported?

A. All incremental revenue becomes available project cash

B. Every in-house operation is necessarily a core competency

C. A positive annual result removes qualification requirements

D. Modeled incremental operating contribution is $54,000, subject to capability and cash-flow validation

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Modeled incremental operating contribution is $54,000, subject to capability and cash-flow validation**

### Why it is correct

The arithmetic gives $54,000 in scoped annual contribution; it is not a discounted investment result or proof of qualified readiness.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Revenue is not cash flow net of costs.
- **B.** Ownership does not prove a differentiating competency.
- **C.** Mandatory qualification cannot be waived by a positive scenario.

</details>

## Question 5

**Question.** A regional node costs $180,000 to launch and models $50,000 annual recurring savings. What arithmetic is justified?

A. A positive NPV is guaranteed

B. Simple payback is 3.6 years if annual savings persist

C. The node qualifies for a tax credit

D. Its risk-adjusted return exceeds every other possible option

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Simple payback is 3.6 years if annual savings persist**

### Why it is correct

The initial $180,000 divided by $50,000 per year is 3.6 years of simple, undiscounted recovery.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** No rate or complete multi-year cash flow was specified.
- **C.** Eligibility for tax incentives is jurisdiction-specific.
- **D.** The scenario contains no comparative risk distribution.

</details>

## Question 6

**Question.** Order-event completeness rises from 140 of 200 orders to 185 of 200. Which conclusion is valid?

A. The number of dashboards is evidence of improved performance

B. Customer retention is now guaranteed

C. Validated event coverage increased from 70% to 92.5%, while service and financial effects still need evidence

D. Long-lead material forecasting is no longer necessary

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Validated event coverage increased from 70% to 92.5%, while service and financial effects still need evidence**

### Why it is correct

The ratios substantiate better coverage, not necessarily realized commercial outcomes.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Dashboard quantity is not the same as trustworthy event coverage.
- **B.** Retention needs customer and commercial evidence.
- **D.** Visibility does not eliminate physical supply lead time.

</details>

## Question 7

**Question.** Customers might avoid $180,000 in downtime while AlderGate would incur $95,000 in service-program cost. What treatment is justified?

A. Retain customer value and company cost as distinct stakeholder measures until value capture is established

B. Book $85,000 immediately as AlderGate net income

C. Book the $180,000 both as company revenue and cost avoidance

D. Disregard workforce obligations because the modeled benefit is high

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Retain customer value and company cost as distinct stakeholder measures until value capture is established**

### Why it is correct

Customer avoided loss is not automatically supplier revenue. A contract, adoption evidence and recognized revenue would be needed.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** The cross-entity arithmetic does not equal company earnings.
- **C.** Double counting misstates economics.
- **D.** Safety and labor duties remain independent gates.

</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** A specialist provider improves complete-on-time delivery from 880 to 955 out of 1,000 orders, at added operating cost. What should AlderGate do?

A. Assume direct customer satisfaction improved by exactly 7.5%

B. Use the premium option for every product

C. Ignore partner continuity because delivery improved

D. Verify the 7.5-percentage-point service improvement, customer value, economics and provider qualification

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Verify the 7.5-percentage-point service improvement, customer value, economics and provider qualification**

### Why it is correct

The service metric supports a 7.5-percentage-point improvement, but the service and partner decision needs economic and eligibility evidence.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Percentage points differ from relative percentages; customer satisfaction is not identical to OTIF.
- **B.** Different service segments may have different willingness to pay.
- **C.** Improved operational results do not replace compliance and continuity controls.

</details>

## Question 9

**Question.** Following an acquisition, AlderGate begins missing customer promises. What is the best first redesign step?

A. Open a regional warehouse immediately

B. Define the measured strategic gap and diagnose process, data, people, capability and partner causes

C. Build inventory of every finished variant

D. Replace the ERP system before checking root cause

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Define the measured strategic gap and diagnose process, data, people, capability and partner causes**

### Why it is correct

A defined target-versus-actual gap and cause-based analysis avoid expensive intervention at the wrong point.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Extra network capacity may not fix qualification or data mismatch.
- **C.** Universal inventory expansion can worsen cash and obsolescence.
- **D.** Software replacement requires an evidenced business requirement.

</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** Three annual network costs are $625,000, $575,000 and $520,000; the last handles only 850 of 1,000 required units/week. What ranking is defensible?

A. The $520,000 design wins because it is cheapest

B. Its throughput shortage can be neutralized by a weighted penalty

C. The $575,000 design is lowest cost among feasible choices under the given assumptions

D. The $625,000 design must win because centralization is safer

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. The $575,000 design is lowest cost among feasible choices under the given assumptions**

### Why it is correct

Mandatory weekly capacity makes the $520,000 option ineligible; the $575,000 option is the lowest among those remaining.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** An infeasible price is not a feasible decision.
- **B.** A hard capacity condition cannot be averaged away.
- **D.** There is no universal rule favoring centralization.

</details>

## Question 11

**Question.** For the model max 40x+55y subject to 2x+4y≤120 hours, x≤40 and y≤25, why is x=40,y=10 a valid solution?

A. It uses 120 hours, respects demand caps, and allocates hours to the higher-contribution-per-hour product first

B. Product B must always dominate because its per-unit contribution is higher

C. The constraint can be ignored after a solver runs

D. Model optimality guarantees all customer and compliance outcomes

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. It uses 120 hours, respects demand caps, and allocates hours to the higher-contribution-per-hour product first**

### Why it is correct

A earns $20/hour versus B's $13.75/hour; 40 A consume 80 hours and 10 B use the remaining 40, producing $2,150 under the specified rules.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** B consumes twice the hours of A per unit.
- **C.** Dropping the capacity limit changes the model.
- **D.** The simplified objective omits real-world gates that still need validation.

</details>

## Question 12

**Question.** A project has 3.60-year simple payback but roughly 4.69-year discounted payback at a positive 10% annual rate. Why?

A. Discounting increases the present value of later receipts

B. The initial investment is omitted from discounted payback

C. Positive NPV always overrides feasibility constraints

D. Later cash inflows have lower present values, so cumulative recovery takes longer

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Later cash inflows have lower present values, so cumulative recovery takes longer**

### Why it is correct

Each later $50,000 benefit is discounted to a smaller present value, extending the modeled recovery period.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** With a positive rate, the present value of later receipts decreases.
- **B.** Initial capital remains part of both payback measures.
- **C.** Positive modeled NPV cannot authorize an infeasible investment.

</details>
