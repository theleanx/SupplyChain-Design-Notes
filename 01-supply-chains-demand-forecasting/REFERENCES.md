# Module 1 Reference Register

The lessons and fictional NorthStar examples are independently written. The following
authoritative public resources support further study and independent verification of
forecasting methods and external demand indicators; they are not source text for the
repository's examples, diagrams, datasets, or assessments.

## Forecasting and statistical methods

- [NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/) — statistical-method reference and worked guidance.
- [NIST time-series analysis section](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section4/pmc4.htm) — time-series definitions, applications, and techniques.
- [NIST time-series methods index](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/dtoc.htm) — moving-average and exponential-smoothing topics in the handbook.

## External demand indicators

- [U.S. Census Bureau Economic Indicators](https://www.census.gov/economic-indicators/) — official construction, trade, retail, wholesale, service, and manufacturing indicators.
- [U.S. Census Manufacturers' Shipments, Inventories, and Orders](https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/current/index.html) — current official manufacturing activity measures.
- [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Price Index](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) — official price-change measures and supporting data.
- [Federal Reserve Industrial Production and Capacity Utilization](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/) — official industrial output and capacity measures.

## Use in this module

External indicators are inputs to a causal demand hypothesis, not automatic forecasts.
Record the series definition, vintage, revisions, lag, affected market, and decision use.
Validate relationships on held-out periods and revisit them after structural change.
