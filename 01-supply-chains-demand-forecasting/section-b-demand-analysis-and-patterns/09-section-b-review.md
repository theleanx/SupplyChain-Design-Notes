# 9. Section B Review — Demand Analysis and Patterns

These original questions test the section's concepts through new scenarios and decision contexts.

## Quick decision rules

- Long-term, uncontrollable external force → **macro environment / strategic response**.
- Internal capability or limitation → **strength or weakness**.
- External favorable/unfavorable condition → **opportunity or threat**.
- Customer need + competitor offer → **market/competitive analysis**.
- Too many SKUs → check **total profit and complexity**, not revenue alone.
- Economy-wide GDP/inflation/interest-rate behavior → **macroeconomics**.
- Individual buyer/firm response to price → **microeconomics**.
- Large quantity response to a small price change → **elastic**.
- Repeating calendar pattern → **seasonality**.
- Multi-year wave with uncertain timing → **cycle**.
- Residual unexplained movement → **random variation**.

## Question 1 — external environment

A manufacturer of premium kitchen appliances expects a significant increase in personal income taxes in its largest market. Which demand implication should planners investigate first?

A. Whether consumer demand for discretionary premium products may weaken  
B. Whether the firm's production yield will automatically improve  
C. Whether supplier capacity will necessarily increase  
D. Whether seasonality will disappear

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** Higher taxes can reduce disposable income, which may affect discretionary demand. B and C do not logically follow from the tax change, and D confuses a structural economic force with calendar-based seasonality.
</details>

## Question 2 — SWOT

NorthStar has proprietary pump-efficiency software, but its service network is weak in a new region. A new water-efficiency regulation is expected to favor high-efficiency pumps. How should these facts be classified?

A. Software = opportunity; service network = threat; regulation = strength  
B. Software = strength; service network = weakness; regulation = opportunity  
C. Software = strength; service network = opportunity; regulation = weakness  
D. All three are external factors

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** The software and service network are internal; the regulation is external. The first helps the company, the second limits it, and the third creates favorable market conditions.
</details>

## Question 3 — market research

Customer interviews show strong interest in a new product feature. Before treating this as an unmet market need, what additional step is most useful?

A. Increase safety stock  
B. Review competing and substitute offerings  
C. Freeze the master schedule  
D. Increase the number of SKUs immediately

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** Interest alone does not prove the need is unsatisfied. Competitive and substitute offerings must be understood before concluding that a market gap exists.
</details>

## Question 4 — portfolio complexity

A consumer-products company adds 40 variants. Revenue rises 4%, but inventory, changeover, packaging, obsolescence, and service costs rise 11%. What should management focus on?

A. Revenue growth only  
B. Total profitability and portfolio complexity  
C. Number of customer requests only  
D. Number of warehouses only

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** More variety is valuable only when incremental revenue and customer value exceed the added total cost and complexity.
</details>

## Question 5 — life cycle

A product's demand is rising rapidly, retailers are frequently out of stock, and quality problems would damage early customer advocacy. Which life-cycle concern is most consistent with the situation?

A. Decline: minimize all availability  
B. Growth: scale availability while protecting quality  
C. Maturity: eliminate capacity  
D. Introduction: assume historical demand is perfectly stable

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** Rapid growth shifts the priority toward sufficient supply and repeatable quality while the business scales.
</details>

## Question 6 — macroeconomics

Current industrial output is weak, but several leading indicators are improving and financing rates have recently declined. Which statement is most reasonable?

A. Future demand must decline because current output is low  
B. The signals may justify scenario planning for recovery before current output turns  
C. Macroeconomic information directly determines the exact SKU forecast  
D. Interest rates have no relevance to capital spending

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** Leading indicators can change before current output. They should inform scenarios, not be treated as a precise SKU forecast.
</details>

## Question 7 — elasticity

A 5% price reduction causes quantity demanded to rise 2%. Which description best fits the observed response?

A. Highly elastic  
B. Relatively inelastic  
C. Perfectly cyclical  
D. Seasonal

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** The quantity response is smaller than the price change; elasticity magnitude is about 0.4.
</details>

## Question 8 — time horizon and elasticity

A commuter continues buying fuel after a price increase but, six months later, moves closer to work and uses public transit. What does this illustrate?

A. Demand can become more elastic over time  
B. Demand always becomes less elastic over time  
C. Seasonality creates price elasticity  
D. Random variation explains all long-term behavior

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** Customers have more time to find substitutes or change behavior over a longer horizon.
</details>

## Question 9 — marginal analysis

A truck is already scheduled to make a route. An extra shipment will generate $500 of revenue and add $180 of fuel, handling, and stop-related cost. The original truck lease and driver's base shift cost are unchanged. What is the marginal economic result?

A. Reject because the full truck cost must be charged again  
B. Accept if no other constraint is violated because incremental benefit is $320  
C. Reject because sunk costs are always included in marginal analysis  
D. Accept only if revenue exceeds the entire historical cost of the truck

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** The decision compares incremental revenue with incremental cost. Costs unchanged by the decision are not relevant to this marginal choice.
</details>

## Question 10 — demand pattern

An ice-cream manufacturer sees demand rise every summer, while the overall annual volume has also been increasing for four years. What two patterns are present?

A. Seasonality and upward trend  
B. Random variation and decline  
C. Cycle and no trend  
D. Inflation and substitution

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** The recurring within-year summer pattern is seasonality; the multi-year direction is a trend.
</details>

## Question 11 — cycle vs. seasonality

Demand for construction machinery rises for several years during an investment boom and then falls for several years. The timing is not tied to a fixed calendar. This is best described as:

A. seasonality  
B. a cycle  
C. random variation only  
D. a promotion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** A multi-year wave with uncertain timing is cyclical rather than seasonal.
</details>

## Question 12 — random variation

A planner has already adjusted historical demand for trend, recurring seasonality, promotions, and a known one-time event. Small irregular fluctuations remain. What should those residual fluctuations normally be treated as?

A. Guaranteed future seasonality  
B. Random variation unless another explainable driver is discovered  
C. A product life-cycle stage  
D. Market share

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** Random variation is the unexplained residual after identifiable drivers are accounted for.
</details>
