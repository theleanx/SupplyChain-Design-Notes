# Module 4: Internal Operations and Inventory

> **Status:** Complete and release-audited. Sections A–D, the Pinehaven integrated capstone, calculations, datasets, and visuals meet the repository's shared content, process-flow, visual, calculation, assessment, navigation, and originality controls.

Module 4 turns supply-and-demand plans into executable internal operations. It follows fictional **Pinehaven Motion Systems**, a manufacturer of industrial servo-drive and controller assemblies, as planners translate aggregate intent into master schedules and material plans, verify capacity, set inventory policy and replenishment controls, protect traceability and accuracy, and improve operating performance through quality and continuous-improvement methods.

All companies, SKUs, quantities, costs, capacities, defect data, diagrams, and review questions are fictional and independently created for learning purposes.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this module, you should be able to:

- explain the manufacturing planning and control hierarchy from S&OP through execution;
- build and interpret master schedules using time fences, projected available balance, and available-to-promise;
- apply MRP logic, BOM explosion, lot sizing, lead-time offsetting, pegging, and exception control;
- connect resource planning, RCCP, CRP, production activity control, and capacity measurement;
- diagnose and respond to load-versus-capacity imbalances without confusing local utilization with system throughput;
- establish inventory policy at aggregate and item levels using ABC, replenishment, safety-buffer, financial, and service evidence;
- apply traceability, configuration, cycle-count, end-of-life, and disposition controls;
- design KPI systems that connect internal measures to customer and financial outcomes;
- choose and use quality tools based on the decision question rather than the chart name; and
- distinguish and combine TQM, lean, JIT, Six Sigma, and theory of constraints appropriately.

## Cross-module handoffs

Module 4 receives demand, forecasting, and supply–demand alignment decisions from Module 1; network, visibility, and performance context from Module 2; and sourcing, supplier, contract, and product-design decisions from Module 3. It converts those upstream decisions into executable production, capacity, inventory, control, and improvement actions.

## How each lesson is structured

Each topic starts with learning objectives and a plain-English explanation, then moves into business relevance, a topic-specific original visual, an operational process flow where appropriate, a Pinehaven scenario, a decision artifact or reproducible calculation, decision logic, trade-offs, failure modes, common confusions, an original knowledge check with distractor rationales, practitioner perspective, related concepts, and sequential navigation.

The module uses **51 original SVG decision visuals** and **15 fictional CSV datasets**. Quantitative examples are governed by the shared formula sheet so the same assumptions and values are reused consistently across lessons and the capstone.
