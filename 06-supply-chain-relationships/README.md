# Module 6: Supply Chain Relationships

> **Status:** Complete and release-audited for v6.0.0. Sections A–B, ClearSpan datasets, calculations, original visuals, section reviews, and the integrated capstone follow the repository's shared learning contract.

Module 6 follows fictional **ClearSpan Automation Systems**, a manufacturer and service provider of industrial automation equipment. The module focuses on the relationships that connect market demand to dependable supply: customer relationship management (CRM), supplier relationship management (SRM), customer service and feedback, supplier performance and certification, strategic alliances, collaboration, stakeholder communication, and cross-cultural relationship management.

The central theme is **relationship intensity must match economic value, operational dependency, and risk**. More contact, more technology, or a deeper alliance is not automatically better. Customer and supplier relationships should be deliberately segmented, measured, governed, and improved. Mandatory process, data, security, certification, ethics, capacity, and continuity gates are applied before economic or weighted ranking where the decision requires them.

All companies, accounts, suppliers, alliance cases, service results, scores, costs, communication records, diagrams, and knowledge checks are fictional and independently created for learning purposes.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this module, you should be able to:

- explain CRM and SRM as relationship operating models rather than only software categories;
- segment customers and suppliers when differentiated treatment changes an operating decision;
- connect customer value, channel preference, relationship lifecycle, VOC, service metrics, and recovery actions;
- evaluate CRM enablement with process, data, and security gates before comparing modeled economics;
- calibrate supplier relationship intensity to criticality, concentration, innovation, customization, lead time, and replaceability;
- distinguish transactional, single-source, sole-source, multisource, collaborative, strategic-alliance, joint-venture, and virtual-organization implications;
- govern supplier performance, certification, corrective action, and remediation with retained evidence;
- apply hard gates before strategic-alliance ranking and maintain an alliance through explicit governance and pulse checks;
- integrate customer and supplier relationships around end-customer outcomes; and
- design communication-management controls that preserve purpose, audience, ownership, acknowledgment, feedback, action closure, and cultural context.

## Cross-module handoffs

Module 6 consumes rather than reteaches the decision methods already established in Modules 1–5:

- [**Module 1**](../01-supply-chains-demand-forecasting/README.md) owns demand management, the Four Ps, product/service life-cycle planning, and supply-demand alignment. Module 6 uses those outputs to shape customer relationship treatment.
- [**Module 2**](../02-network-design-digital-connectivity-performance/README.md) owns enterprise information architecture, integration patterns, analytics, measurement-system design, and broad technology business cases. Module 6 uses those capabilities to enable CRM/SRM and relationship evidence.
- [**Module 3**](../03-sourcing-strategy-product-design-supplier-execution/README.md) owns category strategy, supplier selection, sourcing events, contracting, and procurement execution. Module 6 begins after or alongside those choices and governs the continuing customer/supplier relationship.
- [**Module 4**](../04-internal-operations-inventory/README.md) owns formal quality and continuous-improvement methods. Module 6 uses corrective-action evidence and supplier/customer feedback without reteaching DMAIC or quality-tool mechanics.
- [**Module 5**](../05-forward-reverse-logistics/README.md) owns physical logistics, fulfillment, transportation, trade, and reverse-flow execution. Module 6 uses service and relationship evidence to influence those operating choices without duplicating execution design.

## How each lesson is structured

Each topic contains learning objectives, a plain-English concept, explicit business relevance, a topic-specific original SVG, one operational Mermaid flow, a ClearSpan example, an applied decision artifact or reproducible calculation, retained-evidence guidance, decision logic, trade-offs, common confusion, common mistakes, one original knowledge check with full distractor rationale, practitioner perspective, related concepts, and sequential navigation.
