# Section C Review — Emerging Technology, Implementation, and Change

This original eighteen-question assessment follows the eighteen technology lessons in Module 8. The AlderGate scenarios and numeric values are fictional. Do not confuse a conceptual course description of an emerging technology with provider-specific evidence or verified production performance.

## Review learning outcomes

Evaluate cloud/AI/IoT/blockchain and emerging automation appropriately, screen feasibility before ranking, distinguish roadmap and project controls, select credible tests and cutovers, and demonstrate change adoption and maintenance ownership beyond software deployment.

## Question 1

**Question.** A subscription application is cheaper over three modeled years but cannot meet the local conveyor's required response-time limit. Which approach is defensible?

A. Select SaaS automatically on the three-year figure

B. Remove the physical limit from the business case

C. Keep local time-critical control while assessing cloud visibility separately

D. Assume the provider contract grants unlimited network access

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Keep local time-critical control while assessing cloud visibility separately**

### Why it is correct

Physical response and safety gates must hold; planning/reporting and machine control can have different architectures.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Cost does not authorize unsafe latency.
- **B.** The requirement remains a mandatory operating constraint.
- **D.** Contracts and information sharing still require evidence.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** A press prediction pilot detects 18 of 20 failures and sends 48 alerts. What is its failure recall?

A. 37.5%

B. 90%

C. 84%

D. 48%

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. 90%**

### Why it is correct

Recall counts detected actual failures: 18/20 = 90%. Precision is 18/48 = 37.5%.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** 37.5% is alert precision, not failure recall.
- **C.** 84% is overall classification accuracy.
- **D.** 48 is the number of alerts, not the recall percentage.

</details>

## Question 3

B. Total number of sensor devices purchased

C. Number of colors in exception dashboard

D. Carrier app login count without order identity

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Orders correctly linked to verified events and actioned on time**

Verified order linkage and an authorized timely response connect sensing to actual operational decisions.

</details>

## Question 4

**Question.** An additive manufacturing supplier offers a two-day replacement with incomplete safety qualification. What is its status?

A. Selected due to fastest cycle

B. Automatically greener than stocked parts

C. Approved if a robot handles the part

D. Ineligible until the required physical qualification is verified

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Ineligible until the required physical qualification is verified**

### Why it is correct

A critical part's mandatory material or safety qualification cannot be averaged into lead-time preferences.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Speed cannot replace qualification.
- **B.** Lifecycle evidence is missing.
- **C.** Using robotics does not confer material conformity.

</details>

## Question 6

<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

## Question 7

**Question.** AlderGate's third roadmap phase depends on partner event agreements that are not completed. How should its proposed benefit be treated?

A. Recognized as annual realized revenue

B. Contingent and not yet attributable as an approved operating benefit

C. Included as fully guaranteed ROI

D. Automatically equal to earlier savings

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Contingent and not yet attributable as an approved operating benefit**

### Why it is correct

The prerequisite has not passed, so the phase's financial benefit remains a hypothesis.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **C.** Contingent plans cannot guarantee returns.
- **D.** Benefits must be evaluated independently and avoid double counting.

</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** An IT audit closes 15 of 20 findings but leaves one critical duplicate-order release defect open. What does 75% closure permit?

A. Immediate production release

B. Automatic waiver of user testing

C. Closing all remaining findings administratively

D. Continued hold of the affected release while the critical defect is corrected

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Continued hold of the affected release while the critical defect is corrected**

### Why it is correct

Closure percentage does not supersede an explicit order-integrity gate.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Critical integrity cannot be waived by an aggregate ratio.
- **B.** User acceptance is still required.
- **C.** An unresolved issue cannot be verified closed by relabeling.

</details>

## Question 9

**Question.** Which statement best represents a SMART operational technology requirement?

A. Show verified high-priority exceptions to qualified planners within ten minutes for 95% of relevant events

B. Become the most innovative supply chain

C. Implement a popular dashboard brand

D. Improve supply chain responsiveness someday

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Show verified high-priority exceptions to qualified planners within ten minutes for 95% of relevant events**

### Why it is correct

The statement contains observable action, eligible population, threshold and time boundary.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** An ambition alone is not a measurable acceptance condition.
- **C.** A vendor choice does not define the business requirement.
- **D.** An unspecified future improvement is not a test.

</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** After a gap-analysis pilot, material identity and reporting pass, but mandatory qualified-user access is below target. What follows?

A. Average all scores and approve if the mean is high

B. Buy another reporting tool without root-cause analysis

C. Resolve and retest the failed user-readiness requirement before full affected rollout

D. Remove the target without owner authorization

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Resolve and retest the failed user-readiness requirement before full affected rollout**

### Why it is correct

A mandatory operating capability must pass independently of unrelated improvements.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** An average cannot compensate for a failed hard gate.
- **B.** Replacement software may not solve the access problem.
- **D.** Changing acceptance conditions needs proper governance.

</details>

## Question 11

**Question.** The cheapest vendor has the highest weighted score but fails a mandatory privacy gate. How should it be ranked?

A. First based on the composite score

B. Outside the feasible shortlist until it demonstrates privacy compliance

C. Second after a small penalty is added

D. Eligible if implementation begins early

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Outside the feasible shortlist until it demonstrates privacy compliance**

### Why it is correct

A privacy constraint is an eligibility condition, not a negotiable preference.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A high preference score cannot override confidentiality.
- **C.** A small penalty is not a valid compliance remedy.
- **D.** Early implementation does not repair missing protection.

</details>

## Question 12

**Question.** A cutover test suite passes 190 of 200 scenarios, with two critical release-control tests failing. What is the go-live decision?

A. Launch and rely on support desk tickets

B. Remove the failing scenarios from the report

C. Average failed critical cases with noncritical passes

D. Hold affected automation until critical tests and recovery are verified

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Hold affected automation until critical tests and recovery are verified**

### Why it is correct

A high overall pass percentage cannot allow a failed mandatory quality or access condition.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Support after go-live is not a substitute for readiness.
- **B.** Excluding difficult scenarios misstates the test scope.
- **C.** Critical test significance differs from the aggregate count.

</details>

## Question 13

**Question.** What purpose does a RACI matrix serve in a cross-company integration project?

A. Clarify responsibility, final accountability, consultation and information flow by deliverable

B. Replace the executive charter

C. Eliminate disagreements by disallowing feedback

D. Guarantee that a team reaches the performing stage immediately

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Clarify responsibility, final accountability, consultation and information flow by deliverable**

### Why it is correct

RACI gives specific governance meaning to work assignment and approval rights.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Project authorization and scope still require a charter.
- **C.** Feedback and conflict still need leadership.
- **D.** Team development cannot be mandated by a spreadsheet.

</details>

## Question 14

**Question.** A dependent project path is eight days mapping, five days integration testing and four days acceptance. What is its modeled duration?

A. Eight days

B. Seventeen days

C. Six days

D. Thirteen days

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Seventeen days**

### Why it is correct

The serial path is 8+5+4=17 days. Parallel work does not shorten these dependencies.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** That covers only the mapping task.
- **C.** Six days corresponds to separate parallel hardware in the example.
- **D.** Acceptance cannot be omitted from the governed path.

</details>

Iterative delivery does not relax the mandatory readiness of the functionality released.

## Question 16

## Question 18

**Question.** What must happen when a technology project transitions to normal operations?

A. Stop all funding and monitoring

B. Assign maintenance, emergency recovery, configuration and lifecycle owners with operating metrics

C. Assume every outsourced vendor owns all local devices

D. End support because project acceptance was signed

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Assign maintenance, emergency recovery, configuration and lifecycle owners with operating metrics**

### Why it is correct

Ongoing asset value requires named ownership, maintenance and incident controls after deployment.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Equipment and software still need ongoing support.
- **C.** Responsibility depends on actual service boundaries.
- **D.** Acceptance is the start of routine support, not its end.

</details>
