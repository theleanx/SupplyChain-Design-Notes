# 13. Section A Review — Risk Governance, Identification, and Exposure

## How to use this review

Answer each application question before opening the rationale. These are newly authored training questions using fictional Stonebridge decision settings; they are **not** ASCM exam questions. Passing requires evidence-based explanations, not recognition of glossary phrases.

## Question 1

**Why does a current green supplier-delivery KPI not eliminate supply risk?**

A. It describes observed performance, not every shared failure dependency.

B. It measures total fixed cost rather than supply.

C. It proves unknown threats are more probable.

D. It replaces the need for risk ownership.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** KPI history may conceal untested common dependencies; still map the event path.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **B:** The historical supplier KPI measures actual observed orders and is not a fixed-cost calculation.
- **C:** Novel risk scenarios cannot be assigned higher probability from this KPI alone.
- **D:** A KPI never substitutes for naming risk ownership.

</details>

## Question 2

**A loss-control project is funded but the first exercise fails. What is missing?**

A. A revised corporate forecast.

B. Proof that the response is effective and residual exposure is acceptable.

C. An additional year of supplier sales.

D. Reclassification of every exposure as an opportunity.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** Completion of spending is not effectiveness verification.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** A revised forecast may change demand but does not demonstrate that the failed backup works.
- **C:** Supplier sales history cannot repair the failed exercise.
- **D:** Renaming a control failure as an opportunity does not establish residual protection.

</details>

## Question 3

**Who should be accountable for cross-functional mitigation of a specific connector disruption?**

A. Every participant equally, without one owner.

B. Only the operator that files the supplier scorecard.

C. A named risk owner with supporting quality, supply and partner roles.

D. Any supplier contact with read-only access.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: C.** Distinct execution roles need one clearly accountable owner.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** Shared responsibility without one accountable owner makes the handoff ambiguous.
- **B:** A scorecard operator may provide evidence but need not own continuity approval.
- **D:** Read-only system access does not grant mitigation authority.

</details>

## Question 4

**A newly observed shared IT dependency was not anticipated. Which future action best follows containment?**

A. Record it as a defined known risk, assess consequences and test continuity controls.

B. Keep it forever labeled unknown to avoid estimating exposure.

C. Count the event as an annual probability of exactly 100%.

D. Remove the dependency from the application inventory.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** After discovery, the scenario can be described, owned and reassessed.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **B:** Leaving a discovered dependency as permanently unknown prevents a specific test.
- **C:** One observed incident does not establish a 100% chance every subsequent year.
- **D:** Deleting the dependency would lose precisely the new evidence needed.

</details>

## Question 5

**The board is open to taking product-development risk but forbids uncontrolled life-safety risk. What is this?**

A. Proof all risks have the same tolerance.

B. A threshold that automatically authorizes noncompliance.

C. Inconsistent governance with no remedy.

D. Different appetites by objective and non-negotiable gates.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: D.** Appetite is objective-specific; hard life-safety conditions remain mandatory.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** A uniform tolerance is inconsistent with objective-specific consequence.
- **B:** A threshold breach requires action; it cannot authorize a forbidden action.
- **C:** Distinct risk appetites across objectives are legitimate and can be governed.

</details>

## Question 6

**Which expense should not be multiplied by disruption probability when analyzing response economics?**

A. An incident-only replacement cost.

B. An unavoidable annual preventive service fee paid in every scenario.

C. A contingent incident mobilization payment.

D. A conditional one-time loss after a disruption.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** A certain preventive outlay occurs regardless of whether the loss event happens.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** An incident-only expense is conditional on the disruption occurring.
- **C:** An event-triggered mobilization payment belongs to the conditional loss branch.
- **D:** Conditional one-time loss is weighted by its scenario likelihood.

</details>

## Question 7

**A supply-network partner lacks an active anti-corruption attestation required by policy. What is the right gate outcome?**

A. Proceed if it has the lowest bid.

B. Retain it but hide the gap in supplier notes.

C. Do not treat approval as complete until the required evidence is remediated.

D. Raise the risk appetite in procurement only.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: C.** A mandatory compliance gate cannot be averaged away in weighted scoring.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** A low commercial bid cannot override an obligatory anti-corruption control.
- **B:** Hiding a documented compliance exception impairs decision integrity.
- **D:** Procurement cannot unilaterally redefine a mandatory compliance gate.

</details>

## Question 8

**Which reference assists selection among qualitative interviews, FMEA and simulation?**

A. IEC 31010 assessment-technique guidance.

B. A legal patent certificate.

C. The customer service ratio.

D. An unverified annual insurance brochure.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** The technique-oriented standard discusses the suitability of different assessment methods.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **B:** A patent certificate does not guide assessment-method choice.
- **C:** The customer service ratio is a metric, not a risk-method catalogue.
- **D:** An insurance brochure has no role in selecting validated risk analysis techniques.

</details>

## Question 9

**A shift supervisor reports an untested manual power transfer. What is a sound identification response?**

A. Assign a fabricated 1% annual probability.

B. Suppress the report until a failure occurs.

C. Assume the morning-shift checklist is exhaustive.

D. Document the hypothesis, owner and verification exercise.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: D.** Identification captures a plausible event path and requests verification before quantification.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** A numeric rate from one unverified report is unsupported precision.
- **B:** Suppressing an exposure until it causes harm defeats proactive identification.
- **C:** One checklist from another shift cannot test a live manual-transfer assumption.

</details>

## Question 10

**A risk register has no valid probability estimate for a new event. Which status is appropriate?**

A. Zero likelihood.

B. Not assessed, with evidence requirement and owner.

C. Low automatically because it has never occurred.

D. Closed because it is not quantifiable.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: B.** Unknown estimate is not the same as zero event probability.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** Zero should mean evidence supports no event possibility, not that analysis is pending.
- **C:** Past absence of observation alone does not prove a low probability.
- **D:** A prospective threat should be registered before a monetary loss occurs.

</details>

## Question 11

**Who bears risk of loss for damaged international goods in a given transaction?**

A. Always the legal title holder.

B. Always the insurer.

C. The party determined after checking the actual contract and applicable trade terms.

D. Always the carrier from loading until final customer acceptance.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: C.** Risk transfer must be established under the transaction terms; title and insurance are distinct.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **A:** Ownership and contractual transfer of risk do not automatically coincide.
- **B:** Insurance might compensate losses without determining contractual bearer.
- **D:** A carrier is not necessarily liable for every leg to ultimate acceptance.

</details>

## Question 12

**A subcontractor only needs a binary but has unrestricted access to source code. What is the strongest initial control?**

A. Restrict access to necessary scope, preserve logs and confirm rights.

B. Publish an accusation of misconduct.

C. Disclose more designs so the subcontractor can troubleshoot alone.

D. Assume international IP filings prevent unauthorized access.

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**Correct answer: A.** Least-necessary access and traceable rights address the current exposure.

**Why the other answers are wrong:**

- **B:** Public accusations before investigation may damage evidence and reputation.
- **C:** More source-code disclosure expands the attack surface without contractual need.
- **D:** A filing system does not create automatic access control or worldwide enforceability.

</details>

## Completion and readiness checkpoint

- Score at least **10 of 12** with written justification; this is a fictional recommended study checkpoint, not an official ASCM pass score.
- Demonstrate a risk statement with a condition, event, effect, owner, boundary and horizon; leave unsupported probability blank rather than entering zero.
- Show one governance hard gate (safety, compliance or access), one evidence-verification action, and one partner dependency.
- Explain why reserve availability differs from certain expenditure and why a paid preventive cost is not conditional on a disruption.
- Before proceeding to Section B, confirm that probability, loss severity, residual risk and response alternatives can be assessed using consistent objective, horizon and units.
