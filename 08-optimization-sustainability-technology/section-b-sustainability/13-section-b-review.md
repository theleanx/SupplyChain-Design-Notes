# Section B Review — Sustainability and Standards

These twelve original decisions are based on a **fictional** AlderGate supply chain. They assess the concepts in all twelve Section B lessons, not proprietary certification exam questions. Answer each question before opening the rationale and identify the relevant legal/ethical gate, evidence boundary and date of a named standard.

## Review learning outcomes

Distinguish the triple bottom line, verify short- versus long-term financial claims, calculate emissions and recycling within correct units, judge labor and responsible sourcing evidence, and identify the functions and current editions of the UNGC, OECD, GRI, ISO, SA8000 and ANSI frameworks.

## Question 1

**Question.** A low-cost carrier fails mandatory safety authorization but has attractive carbon and price estimates. What should AlderGate do?

A. Choose it if the weighted score is positive

B. Exclude or remediate it before comparing sustainable options

C. Treat the three TBL indicators as interchangeable

D. Publish its environmental claim as proof of eligibility

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Exclude or remediate it before comparing sustainable options**

### Why it is correct

Mandatory safety authorization is a gate. The TBL frame does not allow a high economic or environmental score to authorize unlawful operation.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A weighted average cannot override an enforceable rule.
- **C.** The three bottom lines have distinct consequences and units.
- **D.** An environmental estimate does not confer safety authorization.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** AlderGate expects five annual $85,000 benefits from a $250,000 upgrade. Which statement is justified?

A. Its NPV must equal $175,000

B. Its future effects are guaranteed by a favorable short-term budget

C. Undiscounted total benefits exceed the initial investment by $175,000, with NPV still undetermined

D. Its labor and safety requirements disappear because nominal savings are positive

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Undiscounted total benefits exceed the initial investment by $175,000, with NPV still undetermined**

### Why it is correct

Five times 85,000 minus 250,000 is 175,000 before discounting; other feasibility tests remain.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** NPV needs dated cash flows and discount rate.
- **B.** Modeled benefit is not guaranteed cash.
- **D.** Positive economics does not waive hard requirements.

</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** A transport study models emissions dropping from 1,860 to 1,200 kg CO2e for one quarter. Which claim is valid?

A. A fictional transport-boundary reduction of 660 kg CO2e, subject to the factor and trip assumptions

B. All cradle-to-grave emissions have dropped by 660 tonnes

C. The organization is now net-zero

D. Any wood packaging may be imported untreated

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. A fictional transport-boundary reduction of 660 kg CO2e, subject to the factor and trip assumptions**

### Why it is correct

The comparison retains the same shipment, mass and distance basis and computes a scoped modeled reduction.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Kilograms and tonnes differ by 1,000; lifecycle is outside the stated boundary.
- **C.** Net zero cannot be inferred from one route.
- **D.** Phytosanitary conditions are independent of a route's modeled footprint.

</details>

## Question 4

**Question.** After AlderGate removes a worker grievance line, complaints fall to zero. What is the strongest interpretation?

A. Worker conditions have been proven perfect

B. Supplier audits can cease

C. A favorable safety score can compensate for retaliation

D. Reporting access may have deteriorated and independent worker evidence is needed

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Reporting access may have deteriorated and independent worker evidence is needed**

### Why it is correct

Silence after removal of a complaint channel is not evidence of safety or absence of rights violations.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A zero count without access is not a verified outcome.
- **B.** Actual labor obligations and control checks continue.
- **C.** Safety harms should not be offset by unrelated scores.

</details>

## Question 5

**Question.** Of four proposed lanes, one lacks a mandatory destination document. Which disposition is correct?

A. Hold or exclude the affected lane pending proof or valid exemption

B. Allow it if an optional sustainability pledge is signed

C. Treat the missing record as a small cost penalty

D. Approve all four because most are documented

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Hold or exclude the affected lane pending proof or valid exemption**

### Why it is correct

A mandatory release condition has not been shown for that route.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Voluntary pledges are not legal documents.
- **C.** Hard compliance is not an economic penalty choice.
- **D.** Each lane's eligibility must be independently established.

</details>

## Question 6

**Question.** The mineral screen has acceptable evidence for 110/120 parts. What does the result mean?

A. Exactly 91.67% of parts are conflict-free

B. Documentation coverage is approximately 91.67%; the remaining ten require inquiry

C. All suppliers must file SEC Form SD

D. Origin may be assumed benign for unknown parts

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Documentation coverage is approximately 91.67%; the remaining ten require inquiry**

### Why it is correct

The fraction reflects evidence readiness, not conflict-free status or legal filing applicability.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Sufficient records do not universally certify origin.
- **C.** Form SD filing depends on specified registrant/product conditions.
- **D.** Unknown origin remains a risk and evidence gap.

</details>

## Question 7

**Question.** A company maps eight of ten internal controls against UN Global Compact areas. What can it claim?

A. It is certified by the UNGC to 80%

B. One strong environment score offsets an unresolved forced labor issue

C. Its internal mapped controls have 80% documentation coverage, without independent certification

D. It is exempt from anti-bribery obligations

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Its internal mapped controls have 80% documentation coverage, without independent certification**

### Why it is correct

The UNGC is a principles initiative, not a certification of hypothetical control completion.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** UNGC does not issue that certificate.
- **B.** Severe rights harms are not compensable by unrelated outcomes.
- **D.** Voluntary participation does not remove legal obligations.

</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** A well-performing vendor receives credible severe subcontractor labor-harm allegations. What does OECD-aligned due diligence prioritize?

A. Annual spending as the only risk driver

B. Price and delivery scores as a complete harm assessment

C. Immediate disengagement in every case regardless of affected workers

D. Assessing adverse impacts, leveraging remediation and tracking real affected-party outcomes

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Assessing adverse impacts, leveraging remediation and tracking real affected-party outcomes**

### Why it is correct

Responsible business conduct evaluates harm to people and environment, not only purchase disruption to the buyer.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Spend is not severity of human-rights harm.
- **B.** Commercial KPIs do not measure labor outcomes.
- **C.** Automatic exit may worsen conditions and requires considered judgment.

</details>

## Question 9

**Question.** A training slide calls GRI 103 'Management Approach' a current Universal standard. What correction is needed?

A. State the current Universal group is GRI 1, 2 and 3 (2021), with GRI 3 covering material topics

B. Treat ISO 26000 as the new GRI 3

C. Conclude that GRI has no Sector Standards

D. Accept the historic labels as necessarily current

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. State the current Universal group is GRI 1, 2 and 3 (2021), with GRI 3 covering material topics**

### Why it is correct

The official revised GRI system uses 2021 Universal Standards 1–3 plus Sector and Topic standards.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** ISO is a distinct standards body and ISO 26000 is guidance.
- **C.** GRI explicitly has Sector Standards.
- **D.** Historic terminology requires an edition check.

</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** A certificate says ISO 14001 but lists a different plant from the one shipping AlderGate's order. What follows?

A. All sites inherit the same certification

B. The certificate must be reviewed for exact edition, scope and site; it does not prove coverage for the order

C. The shipment is approved because the parent company name matches

D. The supplier's product carbon footprint is certified net zero

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. The certificate must be reviewed for exact edition, scope and site; it does not prove coverage for the order**

### Why it is correct

Certification scope and edition matter and may not cover the site supplying the purchase.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Certificate scope is not automatically groupwide.
- **C.** Parent name alone does not prove conformance at a separate site.
- **D.** ISO 14001 does not certify net-zero product footprint.

</details>

## Question 11

**Question.** How should procurement classify an 'ISO 26000 certified' vendor badge?

A. As a verified social responsibility certificate

B. As equivalent to the current SA8000 standard

C. As an inaccurate certification claim: ISO 26000 is guidance and not certifiable

D. As accreditation of the certifying agency

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. As an inaccurate certification claim: ISO 26000 is guidance and not certifiable**

### Why it is correct

ISO itself explicitly rules out certification to ISO 26000.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** This is not a valid certificate under that standard.
- **B.** SA8000 is a separate certifiable program.
- **D.** Accreditation concerns assessment bodies and has separate evidence.

</details>

## Question 12

**Question.** Plant A recycles 8,400/12,000 kg and Plant B recycles 4,800/8,000 kg. Which network disclosure is defensible?

A. 70%, using the larger plant

B. 65%, the simple mean of site percentages

C. 60%, using the lower-performing plant

D. 66%, based on 13,200 recycled kilograms divided by 20,000 eligible kilograms

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. 66%, based on 13,200 recycled kilograms divided by 20,000 eligible kilograms**

### Why it is correct

Mass-weighted aggregation yields 13,200/20,000 = 66% and preserves the two-site reporting boundary.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** One site is not the full population.
- **B.** An unweighted mean ignores unequal waste masses.
- **C.** A single lower-performing site is not the whole network.

</details>

## Completion/readiness checkpoint

A learner is ready to proceed to Section C when they can defend all twelve conclusions without an answer key, independently replay the fictional emissions, percentage and evidence-coverage calculations, and explain **why a current authority/edition check is required** before a supplier or legal claim. Revisit any incorrect questions and the directly linked source documents in the related lesson. No organization, supplier or government body is certified by the exercises.
