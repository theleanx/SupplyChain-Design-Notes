# Module 5: Forward and Reverse Logistics

> **Status:** Integration-complete and release-audited for staging. Sections A–D, HarborPeak datasets, calculations, original visuals, section reviews, and the integrated capstone follow the repository's shared learning contract. Regulatory material is framed with explicit freshness controls.

Module 5 follows fictional **HarborPeak Equipment Systems**, a manufacturer of connected commercial power tools, battery packs, controllers, accessories, and service parts. The module connects the forward movement of goods with the decisions that make that movement economically and operationally viable: logistics strategy, warehousing, materials handling, fulfillment, transportation, cross-border trade, returns, recovery, and waste management.

The central theme is **total-system decision making**. The lowest apparent cost is not automatically the best logistics decision. Service, capacity, capability, security, legal compliance, product integrity, safety, and chain of custody create gates that must be passed before alternatives are ranked economically.

All companies, products, sites, routes, quantities, costs, trade cases, returns, waste streams, diagrams, and knowledge checks are fictional and independently created for learning purposes.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this module, you should be able to:

- connect logistics scope and total-cost thinking to customer-service requirements;
- evaluate 3PL and 4PL models, warehouse ownership, network count/location, capacity, slotting, materials handling, automation, and transportation readiness;
- distinguish warehouse execution activities from value-added distribution services and postponement;
- select fulfillment patterns, distribution architectures, transportation modes, and carrier structures using service gates before cost ranking;
- organize import/export readiness, customs roles, trade documentation, Incoterms® responsibilities, classification, valuation, and trade-zone/agreement decisions;
- distinguish curriculum concepts from current legal or regulatory facts that require authoritative verification;
- design reverse flows for returns, repair, refurbishment, remanufacture, recycling, and responsible disposition;
- evaluate reverse-logistics economics without allowing positive recovery value to override a safety or compliance hold; and
- apply the waste hierarchy, hazardous-waste controls, product stewardship, and waste-exchange concepts to end-of-life decisions.

## How each lesson is structured

Each topic contains learning objectives, a plain-English concept, explicit business relevance, a topic-specific original SVG, an operational flow when the topic is process-oriented, a HarborPeak example, a decision artifact or reproducible calculation, retained-evidence guidance, decision logic, trade-offs, common mistakes, one original knowledge check with full distractor rationale, practitioner perspective, related concepts, and sequential navigation.
