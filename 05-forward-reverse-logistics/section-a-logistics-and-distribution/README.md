# Section A: Logistics and Distribution

Section A builds the logistics operating foundation for Module 5. It starts with the scope and value proposition of logistics, moves through cross-functional integration and provider strategy, then develops warehousing, materials handling, transportation constraints, and forward-looking capacity planning.

This section follows fictional **HarborPeak Equipment Systems**, a manufacturer of connected commercial power tools, battery packs, controllers, accessories, and service parts. All examples, costs, capacity values, provider names, sites, lanes, diagrams, and questions are independently created for learning.

## Learning outcomes

After completing Section A, you should be able to:

- explain logistics as an integrated service-and-total-cost discipline inside supply chain management;
- coordinate logistics objectives and tactics across inventory, warehousing, transportation, information, and product life cycle;
- distinguish 3PL and 4PL models and design provider governance before price ranking;
- choose warehouse ownership, count, location, layout, and capacity using service, cost, control, and risk evidence;
- match mechanized and automated handling systems to the physical flow rather than to technology preference;
- explain transportation movement, temporary storage, capacity constraints, and major logistics trade-offs; and
- build a forward-looking lane/mode capacity plan from demand and known business events.

## How to study the section

Follow the same decision boundary through the lessons: **define service → understand the physical flow → apply hard capability/safety gates → compare total-system economics → assign ownership → monitor the result**. Do not memorize a technology, warehouse type, or provider model as universally superior. The point of Section A is to choose the logistics design that fits the product, customer, network, and risk context.
