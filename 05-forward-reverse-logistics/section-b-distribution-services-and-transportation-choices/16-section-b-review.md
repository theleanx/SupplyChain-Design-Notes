# 16. Section B Review

Use these independently authored questions to test distribution-service, fulfillment, transportation-mode, and carrier-selection reasoning. Choose an answer before opening the rationale.

## Question 1

**Question.** A pallet reaches reserve storage but the WMS location remains the receiving dock. Which control is directly broken?

A. Inventory-location accuracy

B. Mode selection

C. Trade classification

D. Supplier capacity

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Inventory-location accuracy**

### Why it is correct

The physical location and system record no longer agree.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** The issue occurs inside the warehouse.
- **C.** No import/export classification is involved.
- **D.** Supplier capacity does not cause the record mismatch.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** A full inbound load is divided into smaller quantities for several downstream locations. Which distribution service is being performed?

A. Break-bulk

B. Stockpiling

C. Air freight

D. Private carriage

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Break-bulk**

### Why it is correct

Break-bulk divides a large shipment into smaller downstream quantities.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Stockpiling intentionally holds inventory for future demand.
- **C.** Air is a mode, not the warehouse service described.
- **D.** Private carriage describes transport ownership.

</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** Cartons arrive preassigned to outbound routes and bypass normal storage. Which practice best fits?

A. Cross-docking

B. Assortment warehousing

C. Pipeline transport

D. Dedicated production

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Cross-docking**

### Why it is correct

Cross-docking minimizes normal storage between inbound and outbound handling.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Assortment keeps variety near customers rather than bypassing storage.
- **C.** Pipeline is a transportation mode.
- **D.** Production architecture is not the described warehouse flow.

</details>

## Question 6

**Question.** Which statement best separates order channel and fulfillment channel?

A. Order channel captures demand; fulfillment channel physically executes it

B. They are always the same

C. Order channel is a carrier type

D. Fulfillment is only pricing

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Order channel captures demand; fulfillment channel physically executes it**

### Why it is correct

A customer can place an order through one channel while a different physical route fulfills it.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Digital, retail, and sales channels can share or split physical sourcing.
- **C.** Carrier type is a transport structure.
- **D.** Fulfillment includes inventory sourcing and delivery.

</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** The central DC picks a web order, sends it with store replenishment, and the store only holds it for pickup. Which model fits?

A. Flow-through fulfillment

B. Store fulfillment

C. Stockpiling

D. Drop ship

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Flow-through fulfillment**

### Why it is correct

The order is picked upstream and only passes through the store for customer handoff.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Store fulfillment would pick from store stock.
- **C.** The order is already customer-specific.
- **D.** Drop ship would originate at a supplier/manufacturer.

</details>

## Question 9

**Question.** A strategy has the lowest capital cost but fails the mandatory lead-time promise. What is the correct treatment?

A. Remove it from the feasible set unless the requirement/capability changes

B. Let low cost offset the failure

C. Ignore lead time

D. Choose the highest-capital model

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Remove it from the feasible set unless the requirement/capability changes**

### Why it is correct

A hard gate determines feasibility before weighted ranking.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** A mandatory service failure should not be averaged away.
- **C.** Lead time is part of the customer promise.
- **D.** The failure of one option does not prove another by cost alone.

</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** What is the clearest difference between a distribution network and an order-fulfillment channel?

A. The network is the broader system; the channel is one route through it

B. The channel always has more nodes

C. The network includes only suppliers

D. They are synonyms

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. The network is the broader system; the channel is one route through it**

### Why it is correct

A distribution network can contain several fulfillment routes for different customers or products.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Node count can vary in either concept.
- **C.** Distribution networks include downstream nodes and flows.
- **D.** They operate at different scope.

</details>

<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

## Question 12

**Question.** A shipment worth $120,000 occupies 4 m³. What is value density?

A. $30,000/m³

B. $480,000/m³

C. $3,000/m³

D. 30 units/m³

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. $30,000/m³**

### Why it is correct

$120,000 divided by 4 m³ equals $30,000/m³.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** This multiplies instead of divides.
- **C.** This is one order of magnitude low.
- **D.** Units per volume describes packaging density, not value density.

</details>

## Question 13

A. Service, access, security, handling, and reliability can override the density signal

B. It has no useful meaning

C. It only applies to pipeline

D. It already contains lead time

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Service, access, security, handling, and reliability can override the density signal**

### Why it is correct

Value density is one economic lens; feasibility still depends on service and physical constraints.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **C.** It applies to many shipment types.
- **D.** Lead time is a separate input.

</details>

A. When quantified time/risk benefit exceeds the premium and mandatory controls pass

## Question 19

**Question.** Which statement correctly separates carrier class and transportation mode?

A. Carrier class is the operating/legal relationship; mode is the physical movement method

B. Common means rail

C. Private means air

D. They are synonyms

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Carrier class is the operating/legal relationship; mode is the physical movement method**

Common/private/contract/exempt concepts describe carrier structure; rail/motor/water/air describe the physical mode.

## Question 20

**Question.** The lowest-rate carrier fails structural and equipment gates. What should procurement do?

A. Remove it from the qualified ranking

B. Award it because price is lowest

C. Average the failure with price

D. Ignore shipment requirements

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Remove it from the qualified ranking**

### Why it is correct

Eligibility and capability are feasibility gates; cost ranking begins only after they pass.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Price cannot compensate for inapplicability.
- **C.** Mandatory failures should not be averaged away.
- **D.** The shipment requirement defines the service being purchased.

</details>
