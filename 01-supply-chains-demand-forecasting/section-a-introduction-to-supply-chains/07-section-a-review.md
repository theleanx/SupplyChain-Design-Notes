# 7. Section A Review

## High-yield concepts

- A supply chain is a network, not merely a linear sequence.
- Product/service, information, funds, strategic requirements, and reverse flows may move in different directions.
- An echelon is a level/node category in the network.
- Good supply-chain management focuses on net value and balance across participants, not isolated cost reduction.
- Vertical integration emphasizes ownership/control; lateral integration emphasizes specialization and coordinated partners.
- Supply-chain maturity moves from reactive/siloed behavior toward integrated, collaborative, data-driven orchestration.
- Manufacturing and service supply chains use the same core logic even though their entities and outputs can differ.

## Commonly confused concepts

| Concept | Do not confuse with | Key distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Supply chain | Logistics | Logistics is a subset of broader supply-chain management |
| Vertical integration | Lateral integration | Ownership/control vs. specialized partner network |
| Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Functional efficiency vs. enterprise integration |
| Stage 4 | Stage 5 | External collaboration vs. broader systematic orchestration |
| Product flow | Information flow | Physical/service movement vs. planning/status/demand signals |

## Original practice questions

### Question 1

A pump manufacturer has excellent purchasing and transportation teams, but each function optimizes its own cost and they use different planning data. Which maturity description fits best?

A. Multiple dysfunction  
B. Semifunctional enterprise  
C. Integrated enterprise  
D. Orchestrated supply chain

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B. Semifunctional enterprise.** Individual functions have improved efficiency, but enterprise-wide integration is still missing. Stage 3 would require stronger cross-functional process and information integration.
</details>

### Question 2

Which flow normally moves primarily upstream from customers toward suppliers?

A. Finished products  
B. Cash  
C. Primary materials  
D. Product availability

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B. Cash.** Product/material flows generally move toward the customer, while funds generally move back upstream. Information can move in both directions.
</details>

### Question 3

A manufacturer sells a noncore warehousing operation and contracts a logistics specialist that serves many firms. What is the strongest reason this can improve the supply chain?

A. It eliminates the need for coordination  
B. It guarantees lower total cost  
C. It can provide specialized expertise and economies of scale  
D. It converts all fixed cost to profit

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**C.** Lateral supply chains can benefit from partner specialization and scale. They do not eliminate coordination, and lower total cost is not guaranteed.
</details>

### Question 4

A business owns its raw-material source, component plant, assembly plant, and distribution operation. Which structure is most clearly illustrated?

A. Lateral integration  
B. Vertical integration  
C. Cross-docking  
D. Supply-chain orchestration

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B. Vertical integration.** Multiple supply-chain stages are controlled under common ownership.
</details>

### Question 5

A company shares forecasts with a strategic supplier and receives customer sell-through data, but collaboration is limited to a few key external partners. Which maturity stage is most consistent?

A. Stage 1  
B. Stage 2  
C. Stage 4  
D. Stage 5

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**C. Stage 4 - Extended enterprise.** Integration has extended outside the company to selected partners. Stage 5 implies broader, more systematic orchestration and change readiness.
</details>

### Question 6

Which decision best reflects a supply-chain value perspective?

A. Extend supplier payment terms even if a critical small supplier becomes financially unstable  
B. Choose the lowest freight rate regardless of delivery reliability  
C. Select an option that balances total cost, service, cash, and risk across the network  
D. Minimize inventory in every location independently

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**C.** Supply-chain management seeks net value and coordinated performance, not isolated optimization.
</details>

### Question 7

Why might a service company still have a supply chain?

A. Service companies have no suppliers  
B. A service outcome can depend on equipment, labor, information, energy, technology, and external providers  
C. Only manufacturing firms use supply-chain processes  
D. Services do not require demand management

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B.** Service delivery can depend on complex networks of physical and nonphysical inputs.
</details>

### Question 8

Which statement about information flow is most accurate?

A. It only moves downstream with the product  
B. It only consists of purchase orders  
C. It can move both upstream and downstream and includes demand, status, plans, and feedback  
D. It becomes unnecessary in a vertically integrated company

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**C.** Information connects participants in both directions and supports planning and execution.
</details>

### Question 9

A supply network is profitable for the focal manufacturer, but a critical small supplier is being pushed into financial distress by long payment terms. Which perspective is most appropriate?

A. Only the focal company matters because it owns the customer relationship  
B. Supply-chain value should consider affected stakeholders and the sustainability of critical partners  
C. Payment terms are unrelated to supply-chain performance  
D. The supplier should always absorb the financing burden

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B.** A supply chain must create enough value for critical stakeholders to continue participating effectively. A local financial improvement can create larger network risk.
</details>

### Question 10

Several legally independent companies maintain unusually close long-term relationships and limited cross-ownership while remaining separate businesses. Which relationship pattern does this most closely resemble?

A. Full vertical integration  
B. Keiretsu  
C. Internal supply chain  
D. Reverse logistics

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B. Keiretsu.** The organizations cooperate closely but remain legally and economically distinct, unlike a fully vertically integrated structure.
</details>

### Question 11

A company installs advanced software but still has poor data quality, weak partner collaboration, and strong resistance to change. What is the best conclusion about supply-chain maturity?

A. Technology alone proves Stage 5 maturity  
B. The company may still have major maturity gaps despite the technology  
C. It must be vertically integrated  
D. It no longer needs process improvement

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B.** Higher maturity depends on coordinated processes, usable data, people, relationships, resilience, and change capability as well as technology.
</details>

### Question 12

Two regional distribution centers transfer inventory between themselves to rebalance stock. What does this illustrate?

A. Flows can occur between participants at the same echelon  
B. Every supply chain must be linear  
C. Cash always moves downstream  
D. Only suppliers can exchange inventory

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**A.** Real networks can include lateral flows between nodes at the same echelon.
</details>

### Question 13

A company wants to improve the speed with which cash invested in operations returns as customer receipts. Which metric best matches that objective?

A. Market share  
B. Cash-to-cash cycle time  
C. Forecast bias  
D. Capacity utilization

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**B.** Cash-to-cash cycle time focuses on how long operating cash remains tied up before it returns through customer collections.
</details>

### Question 14

A service provider depends on equipment suppliers, cloud systems, trained labor, utilities, and maintenance contractors to serve customers. What is the strongest conclusion?

A. Service businesses do not have supply chains  
B. Only physical goods create supply chains  
C. Service-industry organizations can have complex supply networks even when the final output is intangible  
D. Suppliers matter only in manufacturing

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

**C.** Service outcomes can depend on extensive networks of physical and nonphysical inputs.
</details>
