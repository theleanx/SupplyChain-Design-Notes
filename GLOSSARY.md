# Module 1 Glossary

This glossary provides concise working definitions for the terms used throughout Module 1. Definitions are written for practical planning discussions; individual lessons provide the full context, limitations, and examples.

## A

**Aggregate plan** — A medium-term plan expressed at a product-family or resource level rather than at individual item level.

**Assemble-to-order (ATO)** — A production approach in which common components are prepared in advance and configured into a finished product after demand is known.

**Associative forecasting** — Forecasting that estimates demand using one or more variables believed to influence it.

## B

**Backorder** — Confirmed customer demand that cannot be fulfilled by the requested date and remains open for later delivery.

**Bias** — A persistent forecasting tendency to be predominantly above or below actual demand.

**Bullwhip effect** — Amplification of demand variation as orders and information move upstream through a supply network.

## C

**Capacity** — The output a resource, work center, facility, or supply network can produce during a defined period.

**Chase strategy** — An operations strategy that adjusts capacity or output to follow changes in demand.

**Coefficient of correlation** — A measure of the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables.

**Competitive intelligence** — Systematic collection and interpretation of information about competitors, markets, and strategic moves.

**Constraint** — A resource, rule, material, capability, or policy that limits the feasible plan.

**Cycle** — A wave-like demand movement extending beyond a normal seasonal pattern and often associated with broader business conditions.

## D

**Demand management** — The coordinated process of recognizing, planning, communicating, influencing, and prioritizing demand.

**Demand pattern** — The observable combination of level, trend, seasonality, cycles, events, and random variation in demand history.

**Demand plan** — The organization's agreed statement of expected demand after considering statistical forecasts, market intelligence, commercial actions, and assumptions.

**Demand shaping** — Deliberate action to influence the timing, mix, volume, or profitability of demand.

**Deseasonalizing** — Removing an estimated seasonal effect so that the underlying demand level or trend can be analyzed.

## E

**Engineer-to-order (ETO)** — A production approach in which significant engineering work begins after a specific customer requirement is received.

**Environmental scan** — Structured review of external political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental forces.

**Exponential smoothing** — A forecasting method that updates the prior forecast using a smoothing factor applied to the most recent error.

## F

**Fill rate** — The percentage of requested demand fulfilled immediately from available supply, usually measured by units, lines, or orders.

**Forecast** — An estimate of future demand based on available evidence and stated assumptions.

**Forecast error** — The difference between actual demand and forecast demand for the same period.

**Forecast horizon** — The future time span covered by a forecast.

## H

**Hybrid strategy** — An operations strategy that combines level output, capacity adjustments, inventory, backlog, external supply, or demand shaping.

## L

**Lagging indicator** — A measure that confirms a change after the underlying business condition has already shifted.

**Lateral integration** — Coordination or combination with organizations operating at a similar stage of a value chain.

**Leading indicator** — A measure that may signal a future change before it appears in the primary outcome.

**Level strategy** — An operations strategy that keeps output relatively stable and absorbs demand variation through inventory, backlog, or other buffers.

## M

**Make-to-order (MTO)** — A production approach in which product completion is initiated by a specific customer order.

**Make-to-stock (MTS)** — A production approach in which finished goods are produced in advance based on expected demand.

**Mean absolute deviation (MAD)** — The average absolute forecast error across a defined set of periods.

**Mean absolute percentage error (MAPE)** — The average absolute forecast error expressed as a percentage of actual demand.

**Mean squared error (MSE)** — The average of squared forecast errors, which gives greater weight to larger misses.

**Moving average** — A forecast calculated from the average of a selected number of recent observations.

## N

**New-product introduction (NPI)** — The coordinated process of preparing a new product for commercial launch and operational execution.

## O

**Operations plan** — A time-phased statement of the output, inventory, workforce, capacity, and service response required to support the agreed plan.

**Order penetration point** — The point in the supply process where a specific customer order becomes linked to materials or capacity.

## P

**PDCA** — Plan–Do–Check–Act, a recurring cycle for testing action, evaluating results, and improving the next decision.

**Planning horizon** — The future interval over which plans, demand, supply, and constraints are evaluated.

**Postponement** — Delaying final product differentiation or commitment until more reliable demand information is available.

**Product life cycle** — The progression of a product through introduction, growth, maturity, and decline, with different demand and supply implications at each stage.

**Product portfolio** — The collection of products, services, families, and variants managed as a set of strategic and economic choices.

**Production-to-order (PTO)** — A broad order-driven approach in which production activity is authorized in response to customer demand.

## R

**Random variation** — Demand movement that cannot be explained reliably by known patterns or drivers.

**Regression** — A statistical method that estimates the relationship between an outcome and one or more explanatory variables.

**Reseasonalizing** — Reapplying the expected seasonal effect to a forecast of the underlying demand level.

**Resource plan** — A long- or medium-range check that determines whether critical resources can support the business or operations plan.

## S

**Safety stock** — Inventory maintained to reduce the risk created by demand or supply uncertainty.

**Sales and operations planning (S&OP)** — A recurring cross-functional process that reconciles demand, supply, financial expectations, risks, and decisions at an aggregate level.

**Seasonality** — A demand pattern that repeats at predictable calendar intervals.

**Service level** — A defined measure of the organization's ability to meet customer demand or avoid a shortage.

**Statistical forecast** — A forecast generated from quantitative patterns or relationships in data before judgmental adjustments.

**Supply chain** — A network of organizations, resources, processes, and flows that creates and delivers value to a customer.

**Supply review** — A cross-functional evaluation of capacity, materials, inventory, constraints, risks, and response options against the demand plan.

**SWOT** — A structured view of internal strengths and weaknesses alongside external opportunities and threats.

## T

**Tracking signal** — A ratio comparing cumulative forecast error with MAD to identify sustained bias.

**Trend** — A persistent long-term upward or downward movement in demand.

## V

**Value flow** — The progression through which activities, capabilities, and exchanges increase usefulness for the customer and participating organizations.

**Vertical integration** — Ownership or control of additional upstream or downstream stages in a value chain.
