# 10. Section A Review and Original Practice

Use these questions to test whether you can connect strategy, network choices, information, investment, maturity, and governance.

## Rapid review

You should be able to:

- write a measurable network-design brief;
- segment demand before selecting policies;
- compare network alternatives using scenarios and sensitivity analysis;
- distinguish efficiency, responsiveness, and resilience;
- evaluate total delivered sourcing economics;
- define decision-centered information requirements;
- calculate benefit-cost ratio, ROI, and payback;
- sequence capabilities by dependency; and
- assign decision rights and exception ownership.

## Original knowledge checks

### 1. Strategy translation

Which statement is a design requirement rather than a broad aspiration?

A. Become the market leader
B. Improve customer experience
C. Deliver priority spare-part lines complete within 24 hours while holding no more than 30 inventory days
D. Digitize the supply chain

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** It defines a measurable service and inventory boundary.
</details>

### 2. Segmentation

A segment is operationally useful only when it changes what?

A. The product name
B. A policy, decision, or service promise
C. The annual report
D. The legal entity

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Useful segmentation produces differentiated operating decisions.
</details>

### 3. Network scope

Why should reverse flows be included in network design?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Returns, repair, recovery, disposal, and reusable packaging consume nodes, capacity, transportation, information, and cash. Ignoring them understates cost and capability requirements.
</details>

### 4. Weighted score

An alternative receives scores of 4 for cost, 5 for service, and 2 for implementation. Weights are 40%, 40%, and 20%. What is the weighted score?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`(4×0.40) + (5×0.40) + (2×0.20) = 4.0`.
</details>

### 5. Sensitivity

Why test different weights in a network scorecard?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Weights represent judgment. Sensitivity testing shows whether the recommendation is robust or depends on one narrow preference set.
</details>

### 6. Efficiency

Can a high-utilization network be operationally inefficient?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Yes.** High utilization may create queues, expedites, missed service, and low throughput when variability is present.
</details>

### 7. Resilience

Why is an unqualified backup supplier not an executable resilience option?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Specifications, quality approval, capacity, commercial terms, data, and switching procedures may not be ready when needed.
</details>

### 8. Total delivered cost

A component costs $80, with $7 freight, $3 duties, $5 carrying cost, and $4 expected quality/administrative cost. What is its adjusted delivered cost before risk scenarios?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`$80 + $7 + $3 + $5 + $4 = $99`.
</details>

### 9. Shared exposure

Two direct suppliers use the same critical sub-tier producer. What risk remains?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Concentration remains at the shared sub-tier source.
</details>

### 10. Information latency

What four clocks should be distinguished for an operational event?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Event, capture, availability, and action time.
</details>

### 11. Update frequency

Should every supplier record be exchanged in real time?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No. Frequency should match decision value, required freshness, risk, and lifecycle cost.
</details>

### 12. Benefit-cost ratio

Three-year benefits are $540,000 and costs are $450,000. What is the ratio?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`$540,000 / $450,000 = 1.20`.
</details>

### 13. ROI

Using the same figures, what is ROI?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`($540,000 - $450,000) / $450,000 = 20%`.
</details>

### 14. Capability sequence

Why should managed exceptions normally precede automated decisions?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Managed exceptions establish definitions, ownership, response evidence, and control boundaries that automation needs.
</details>

### 15. Governance

What is the difference between consultation and accountability?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Many roles may provide input, but one named role must own approval or outcome accountability.
</details>
