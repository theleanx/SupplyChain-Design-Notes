# 10. Section B Review

Use these independently authored questions to test operating decisions, calculations, and concept boundaries. Choose an answer before expanding the rationale.

## Question 1

**Question.** Which capacity check is aligned with the master production schedule?

A. Resource planning

B. Rough-cut capacity planning

C. Cycle counting

D. Physical inventory

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**B. Rough-cut capacity planning**

### Why it is correct
RCCP tests whether key resources can support the MPS before the schedule drives more detailed planning.

### Why the other answers are wrong
Resource planning is longer range and aggregate. Cycle counting and physical inventory are inventory-control activities.
</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** What does a resource profile add to long-range capacity planning?

A. A time-phased view of resource load

B. A list of customer invoices

C. A count of finished-goods locations only

D. A daily dispatch sequence

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**A. A time-phased view of resource load**

### Why it is correct
A resource profile translates the production plan into resource requirements by period so the gap date becomes visible.

### Why the other answers are wrong
B and C are unrelated records; D belongs near execution rather than long-range resource planning.
</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** Which capacity strategy deliberately adds increments before forecast demand reaches existing capability?

A. Stepwise lead

B. Stepwise lag

C. No-change strategy

D. Backflush strategy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**A. Stepwise lead**

### Why it is correct
A stepwise lead strategy stages capacity additions while remaining ahead of forecast demand.

### Why the other answers are wrong
Lag adds after demand catches up; backflushing is an inventory-recording technique; no-change does not describe capacity expansion.
</details>

## Question 4

**Question.** Pinehaven's Q4 Burn-in Test requirement is 2,500 standard hours and current capability is 2,100. What should RCCP conclude before the MPS is released unchanged?

A. The resource has 400 hours of spare capacity

B. The critical resource has a 400-hour gap that requires schedule or capacity action

C. CRP should be skipped

D. The plant is feasible because another department may have spare hours

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**B. The critical resource has a 400-hour gap that requires schedule or capacity action**

### Why it is correct
Critical-resource feasibility is the purpose of RCCP; unrelated spare capacity does not make a specialized bottleneck feasible.

### Why the other answers are wrong
A reverses the sign; C removes a later detailed check; D assumes resources are interchangeable without evidence.
</details>

## Question 5

**Question.** Which information combination is most appropriate for detailed CRP?

A. Open orders, planned releases, routings, and work-center data

B. Annual revenue and corporate brand value

C. Only the demand forecast

D. Only demonstrated capacity

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**A. Open orders, planned releases, routings, and work-center data**

### Why it is correct
CRP needs detailed work plus the routing and work-center standards that translate orders into load.

### Why the other answers are wrong
B is not a work-center load basis. C and D omit essential parts of the detailed capacity calculation.
</details>

## Question 7

**Question.** Final Assembly has 400 available hours and works 360 hours. What is utilization?

A. 90%

B. 105%

C. 111.1%

D. 378%

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**A. 90%**

### Why it is correct
Utilization is 360 / 400 × 100 = 90%.

### Why the other answers are wrong
105% is the efficiency value in the Pinehaven example; the other percentages use incorrect relationships.
</details>

## Question 8

**Question.** Final Assembly produces 378 standard hours while consuming 360 actual hours. What is efficiency?

A. 90%

B. 95%

C. 100%

D. 105%

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**D. 105%**

### Why it is correct
Efficiency is standard output divided by hours actually worked: 378 / 360 = 1.05.

### Why the other answers are wrong
The other values do not match the defined efficiency calculation.
</details>

## Question 9

**Question.** A work center has 240 available hours, 87.5% utilization, and 105% efficiency. What is rated capacity?

A. 183.75 standard hours

B. 210.00 standard hours

C. 220.50 standard hours

D. 252.00 standard hours

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**C. 220.50 standard hours**

### Why it is correct
Rated capacity is 240 × 0.875 × 1.05 = 220.50 standard hours.

### Why the other answers are wrong
The alternatives omit or misapply one of the utilization/efficiency factors.
</details>

## Question 10

**Question.** Why compare demonstrated capacity with rated capacity?

A. To replace all standards permanently

B. To compare calculated capability with historically sustained output

C. To calculate customer revenue

D. To determine inventory ownership

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**B. To compare calculated capability with historically sustained output**

### Why it is correct
Demonstrated capacity provides a practical historical reference that can reveal optimistic or stale assumptions in rated capacity.

### Why the other answers are wrong
A overstates the role of history; C and D are unrelated to work-center capacity measurement.
</details>

## Question 11

**Question.** A one-week overload is expected to disappear next week. Which action should be evaluated before permanent hiring?

A. Approved overtime or another reversible short-term response

B. A new plant

C. Producing inventory without demand

D. Ignoring customer commitments

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**A. Approved overtime or another reversible short-term response**

### Why it is correct
Temporary gaps should first be tested against reversible responses when service, quality, fatigue, and cost remain acceptable.

### Why the other answers are wrong
B is structural overreaction; C creates excess inventory; D fails the service objective.
</details>

## Question 12

**Question.** Subcontracting a portion of work from an overloaded internal work center primarily does what?

A. Increases the internal machine's physical capacity

B. Reduces the load assigned to the internal resource

C. Raises utilization automatically

D. Eliminates the need for quality control

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**B. Reduces the load assigned to the internal resource**

### Why it is correct
The work is moved outside the internal resource, so its assigned load falls; the machine itself has not become more capable.

### Why the other answers are wrong
A confuses load with capacity; C is not guaranteed; D is false because external work still requires governance and quality controls.
</details>

## Question 13

**Question.** Burn-in Test is the current system constraint. What is the strongest response to an upstream work center with spare capacity?

A. Produce as much as possible upstream

B. Release only enough work to protect the constraint and required buffer

C. Ignore the constraint because upstream capacity is higher

D. Increase WIP until all floor space is full

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**B. Release only enough work to protect the constraint and required buffer**

### Why it is correct
Overfeeding a constraint increases queue and cycle time without increasing system throughput.

### Why the other answers are wrong
A, C, and D optimize local activity rather than system flow.
</details>

## Question 14

**Question.** What is the purpose of a kanban or equivalent pull signal?

A. Authorize replenishment or movement based on downstream consumption/readiness

B. Forecast annual demand

C. Maximize every local batch size

D. Replace all production planning

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer
**A. Authorize replenishment or movement based on downstream consumption/readiness**

### Why it is correct
A pull signal controls when replenishment or movement is authorized based on actual need at the consuming point.

### Why the other answers are wrong
B is forecasting; C works against small controlled flow; D overstates the role of kanban.
</details>
