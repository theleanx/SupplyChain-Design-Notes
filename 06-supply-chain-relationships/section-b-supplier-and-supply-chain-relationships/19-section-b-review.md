# 19. Section B Review

Use these independently authored questions to test supplier, alliance, collaboration, communication, and end-to-end relationship judgment. Choose an answer before opening the rationale.

## Question 1

**Question.** What is the best reason to use deeper SRM governance?

A. The supplier sends many invoices

B. Every supplier should be a strategic partner

C. Dependency, criticality, customization, innovation, or risk justify richer relationship management

D. The supplier uses the same ERP vendor

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Dependency, criticality, customization, innovation, or risk justify richer relationship management**

### Why it is correct

SRM intensity should fit the role and value/risk of the supplier relationship.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Transaction volume is not sufficient.

- **B.** Routine suppliers may be better served transactionally.

- **D.** Technology commonality alone does not define relationship depth.

</details>

## Question 2

**Question.** Why is Prairie Packaging modeled as transactional while BlueMesa is strategic?

A. Packaging is always unimportant

B. Transactional means poor supplier performance

C. Strategic means highest spend only

D. The fictional evidence shows very different criticality, risk, customization, lead time, and alternatives

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. The fictional evidence shows very different criticality, risk, customization, lead time, and alternatives**

### Why it is correct

Supplier segmentation combines relationship-relevant evidence rather than prestige or spend alone.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Packaging can be critical in other contexts.

- **C.** Spend is only one possible factor.

- **B.** Transactional describes the appropriate relationship intensity, not supplier quality.

</details>

## Question 3

**Question.** What is the strongest justification for supplier co-location?

A. A measurable high-value coordination problem can be reduced by proximity and the governance risks are controlled

B. The supplier requests office space

C. All strategic suppliers must be co-located

D. Co-location removes IP risk

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. A measurable high-value coordination problem can be reduced by proximity and the governance risks are controlled**

### Why it is correct

Deeper integration should solve a real coordination need and account for access, dependency, and control.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **B.** Convenience alone is not sufficient.

- **C.** Strategic suppliers can need different integration depth.

- **D.** Proximity increases, not eliminates, information-control needs.

</details>

## Question 5

**Question.** What best distinguishes the relationship implication of strategic sourcing from tactical buying?

A. Tactical buying has no controls

B. Strategic sourcing always means sole sourcing

C. Strategic relationships usually require broader information sharing and cross-functional governance when the supply need justifies it

D. Strategic sourcing eliminates purchase orders

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. Strategic relationships usually require broader information sharing and cross-functional governance when the supply need justifies it**

### Why it is correct

The key Module 6 distinction is the depth and scope of the ongoing relationship.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Tactical buying still requires reliable controls.

- **D.** Routine transactions may remain part of strategic relationships.

</details>

## Question 6

**Question.** One supplier gets all current volume but a qualified alternate exists. What is the sourcing relationship?

A. Sole source

B. Joint venture

C. Multisource

D. Single source

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Single source**

### Why it is correct

Single sourcing is a deliberate concentration choice when alternatives exist.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Sole source means no practical alternative is available.

- **C.** Multisourcing uses more than one independent supplier.

- **B.** A joint venture is a collaboration structure.

</details>

## Question 7

B. No one can communicate

D. The supplier automatically becomes certified

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**A. Inconsistent commitments can emerge unless decision rights and escalation are defined**

### Why it is correct

More direct functional interfaces increase communication richness and governance complexity.

### Why the other answers are wrong

</details>

## Question 9

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Institutionalization embeds the alliance in organizations rather than relying on personal chemistry.

## Question 10

**Question.** What is the purpose of an alliance pulse check?

A. Re-run the original supplier selection score every month

B. Avoid change

C. Replace formal governance

D. Assess relationship health as well as business results and adapt before problems become severe

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. Assess relationship health as well as business results and adapt before problems become severe**

Pulse checks create early warning on trust, decision quality, commitment, and collaboration.

</details>

A. An aggregate can hide critical weaknesses in delivery, quality, or corrective action

D. Only price matters

<details>

## Question 12

**Question.** A supplier has a high audit score but a critical certification failure. What should happen?

A. Certify based on the average

B. Apply the hard-fail rule defined for the certification control

C. Ignore certification because performance is enough

D. Change the threshold after seeing the supplier

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Apply the hard-fail rule defined for the certification control**

### Why it is correct

Critical gates are separate from the average audit score.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Averages can hide unacceptable failures.

- **C.** Certification and performance provide different assurance evidence.

- **D.** Changing rules to favor a supplier undermines governance.

</details>

## Question 13

**Question.** When is a corrective action truly closed?

A. When the supplier emails a response

B. When the due date passes

C. When implementation and effectiveness are verified according to the agreed evidence

D. When a buyer stops asking about it

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**C. When implementation and effectiveness are verified according to the agreed evidence**

### Why it is correct

Closure requires evidence that the correction worked, not only that activity occurred.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** A response is not effectiveness evidence.

- **B.** An overdue date indicates a control problem, not closure.

- **D.** Silence does not resolve the issue.

</details>

## Question 14

**Question.** What is the central purpose of CRM-SRM integration?

A. Put customer and supplier data in one file

B. Eliminate planning

## Question 16

**Question.** What is a classic collaboration barrier?

A. Aligned incentives

B. Suboptimization in which one party improves a local measure at the expense of the supply chain

C. Clear shared objectives

D. Verified feedback

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**B. Suboptimization in which one party improves a local measure at the expense of the supply chain**

### Why it is correct

Collaboration can fail when local incentives reward behavior that harms the shared outcome.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** Aligned incentives support collaboration.

- **C.** Clear objectives are a foundation, not a barrier.

- **D.** Verified feedback improves communication.

</details>

## Question 18

**Question.** How should cultural-dimensions models be used?

A. As deterministic rules for individuals

B. To avoid direct stakeholder feedback

C. To rank national cultures

D. As hypotheses that prompt research, respectful questions, local expertise, and adaptation

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

### Correct answer

**D. As hypotheses that prompt research, respectful questions, local expertise, and adaptation**

### Why it is correct

Broad cultural frameworks can prepare teams for possible differences but cannot predict individuals.

### Why the other answers are wrong

- **A.** This creates stereotypes.

- **C.** The purpose is understanding and adaptation, not ranking.

- **B.** Direct evidence is the most important validation step.

</details>
